1. National Sustainable Mobility Policy and Action Plan

The National Sustainable Mobility Policy (SMP) was published in April 2022 and sets out a strategic framework to 2030 for active travel (walking and cycling) and public transport journeys to help Ireland meet its climate obligations, as well as its social and economic objectives. It was accompanied by an Action Plan to 2025 which contained actions to improve and expand sustainable mobility options across the country by providing safe, green, accessible and efficient alternatives to car journeys.

The 91 actions in the SMP Action Plan 2022-2025 are grouped under 10 high-level Goals, all stemming from the SMP’s three overarching principles:

Safe and Green Mobility

Key Principle

1. Improve mobility safety.

2. Decarbonise public transport.

3. Expand availability of sustainable mobility in metropolitan areas.

4. Expand availability of sustainable mobility in regional and rural areas.

5. Encourage people to choose sustainable mobility over the private car.

People-Focused Mobility

Key Principle

6.Take a whole of journey approach to mobility, promoting inclusive access for all.

7.Design infrastructure according to Universal Design Principles and the Hierarchy of Road Users model.

8.Promote sustainable mobility through research and citizen engagement.

Better-Integrated Mobility

9. Better integrate land use and transport planning at all levels.

Key Principle

10. Promote smart and integrated mobility through innovative technologies and development of appropriate regulation.

Progress Reports have been published on an annual basis detailing the delivery of each of the Actions set out in the Plan. This Final Progress Report marks the conclusion of the 2022–2025 SMP actions Plan, covering the period April to December 2025.

### 4. Moving Together: A Collaborative Approach to Systems Change in Transport 2026–2030

The Moving Together Strategy was published in March 2026, following a commitment in the Climate Action Plan 2023 (CAP23). The aim of the Strategy is to help manage transport demand and congestion, and to support and reinforce the objectives of other transport policies, including the National Sustainable Mobility Policy. It represents a call for collective action across Government and society, not only to reduce carbon emissions over the medium to long term, but also to address the more immediate challenges of congestion and air quality arising from private traffic and freight movements. The Strategy will help unlock the full potential of the Government’s investment in sustainable mobility by prioritising these modes in urban centres, in line with commitments in the Programme for Government.

The development of the Strategy has been informed by extensive research and analysis of a wide range of case studies both nationally and internationally. It has also been shaped by broad stakeholder and public engagement, with contributions received from over 100 stakeholders and 57 organisations. Six expert subgroups were established as part of the Strategy’s development. Their inputs, together with the wider research and evidence base, underpin the Strategy’s recommendations and actions. The Strategy includes 64 actions, designed collectively to improve transport system efficiency and reduce congestion over the medium to long term. In line with its development, the Strategy will be implemented with four key principles in mind: Just Transition; Accessibility and Inclusion; Rural Recognition; and Economic and Financial Sustainability.

The Strategy seeks to encourage a collective and collaborative approach to behavioural change, in an effort to reduce or avoid the need for unsustainable travel - insofar as possible - across all areas of the economy and society. It is also expected to have a key role in reducing congestion and air pollution, and in meeting the CAP23 commitment of reducing total vehicle kilometres travelled, both private and commercial, by 20%.

The Strategy will now progress to the implementation phase, supported by a strong oversight mechanism to monitor the delivery of actions and assess the effectiveness of the measures outlined. This will complement and reinforce the significant investment being made in public transport services and active travel infrastructure, supporting a shift towards more sustainable modes such as walking, cycling, and public and shared transport.

### 5. SMP Action Plan 2026-2030

The SMP Action Plan 2026–2030 succeeds the previous Action Plan. The new Plan maintains the collaborative approach established under the Sustainable Mobility Policy and integrates with the Programme for Government 2025 as well as wider government policy. It was developed through a structured and inclusive consultation process, designed to ensure that the measures contained within it are informed by evidence, stakeholder expertise, and lived experience across the transport and mobility system.

alongside relevant Government Departments and agencies, to refine high-level proposals into a coherent and implementable set of actions. This included collaboration with the Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage, reflecting the SMP’s emphasis on better integrating transport with land use and spatial planning as well as building on the establishment of the Housing Activation Office with embedded transport expertise. The development process also expanded collaboration to include the Department of Further and Higher Education, Research, Innovation and Science in relation to the transport needs of third-level students; the Department of Finance in respect of measures such as the Cycle to Work scheme; and the Department of Climate, Energy and the Environment on areas of shared interest.

A cornerstone in the development process was the National Sustainable Mobility Forum. The 2025 Forum was held over four halfday events, two in person and two online, to facilitate equity of participation from a broad range of stakeholders. Approximately 160 stakeholders across the sustainable mobility sector including Local Authorities, transport agencies, Disabled People’s Organisations, representatives of Government departments, members of Public Participation Networks and other community representatives, youth groups and academics took part in the Forum events. The insights of these stakeholders were captured in the Forum report and fed into the development process for the new Action Plan.

The resulting SMP Action Plan 2026–2030, published in March 2026, sets out 95 actions designed to further advance sustainable mobility across infrastructure, services, spatial planning, accessibility, behaviour change, and data. The Plan aligns closely with wider national and international policy frameworks, including the Programme for Government 2025, the National Development Plan Review 2025, the revised National Planning Framework, and key sectoral strategies. Together, these actions will build on the success of its predecessor in delivering a more sustainable transport system for all.
