## Item 8 — Referrals from Municipal District meetings and Full Council: {#item-8}

\- Traffic Calming Policy — Councillor Duffy (Kildare-Newbridge MD) - Yellow boxes — Councillor Tracey O’Dwyer (Kildare-Newbridge MD) - Bicycle counters — Councillor Bill Clear (Full Council Traffic Calming Policy Daragh Conlan, Acting Senior Engineer gave a short briefing on the Traffic Calming Guidance Document adopted by Kildare County Council in September 2020. The aim of the Policy is to address the concerns that the Public and Public Representatives have regarding the negative impact of excessive or inappropriate traffic speed on public roads. The objectives of the Policy are to e Improve safety e Reduce and control vehicular speed « Improve driver awareness of vulnerable road users. Mr. Conlan outlined that following initial assessment of potential traffic calming schemes and requests, a series of seven measures are considered before installation of traffic calming measures. 1 Additional Signage 2 Additional Road Lining 3 Driver Feedback Signs 4 Bollards 5 Reduced carriage width 6 Priority System 7 Vertical Deflection The seventh measure will only be implemented after the first six measures have been successfully met. Councillor Kevin Duffy noted the general perception that there is a sentiment that the Council is not prioritising traffic calming measures, and he has not observed any such measures implemented in the past seven years. He suggested that an open discussion on this issue is needed. He expressed the view that current signage adds little value. It was highlighted that residents have concerns about noise caused by vehicles hitting speed ramps. Councillor Tracey O'Dwyer commented that ramps may not always be the best solution, citing two examples: one location where ramps are effective and another where they are not. She queried whether installing ramps in large open spaces is ineffective. Councillor Paul Ward spoke about the positive impact of a “Welcome Sign” combined with a speed ramp at the entrance to Kilcock village. He asked whether developers of new estates are permitted to install speed ramps as part of their developments. Councillor Ward also questioned who completes the Traffic Calming Priority Assessment Form and suggested that the form might need to be revised and circulated to residents for their input. Councillor O’Dwyer raised the idea of developing a suite of measures to address traffic calming in full. In response Mr. Chris Galvin clarified that speed ramps are the issue being referred to and said that the problem is not entirely the Councils. An Garda Siochana do not have the resources to address the issues. As a local authority a conversation needs to be had around the guidelines and whether they are there to help or to hinder. Mr. Galvin proposed using a location as a pilot project and to review the policy and this was agreed by the Committee. Councillor O’Dwyer’s input was noted to keep this in mind when decisions are being taken. Councillor Ward’s point was noted. Mr. Conlan responded that developers are not installing speed ramps but are instead incorporating meandering road layouts and cul-de-said that that he does not see any issue with using the form and will work with Councillor Ward in this regard. He said that the Council has invested in several new traffic counters, which are also utilised to develop restoration Improvement and restoration maintenance projects and to address related queries. He agreed that Councillor O’Dwyer’s queries would be examined with the Chief Technician and revisit the implementation of the policies. Yellow Boxes Mr. Daragh Conlan, A/Senior Engineer briefed the members about the Policy on Yellow Boxes outlining there are three criteria for the implementation of a yellow box: 1. Evidence of all day blocking and significant queues 2. Side road traffic flows should be greater than 5000 AADT 3. Not permitted for private residences Councillor O’Dwyer referenced two locations where yellow boxes near junctions have negatively affected traffic flow and stated that they should be removed. She asked whether the policy could be amended to reflect this Councillor Bill Clear referred to the first of the three criteria, noting that there is no road subject to all-day blocking, and expressed the view that this requirement is a cop-out. In response, Mr. Conlan, responded as the Policy is in place and not open to amendment but requested that Councillor O'Dwyer to send in her observations in this regard to review same. Councillor Clear’s observations were noted. Bicycle Counters Councillor Bill Clear spoke positively about the Public using Greenways and put forward question regarding installation of traffic counters on the Royal Canal Greenway. Donal Hodgins, Senior Engineer, Sustainable Transport responded that in order to ensure consistency across county borders and along national routes such as Greenways, counters would be the responsibility of Waterways Ireland or the National Transport Authority/Transport Infrastructure Ireland and not the Local Authority. The NTA has installed counters along the Royal Canal Scheme in north County Kildare and are maintaining the equipment and data collection. Following further discussion, it was agreed that the Council would write to the three national entities calling for a network of cycle counters to be installed and maintained. Councillor Bill Clear was thanked for his attendance and left the meeting.
