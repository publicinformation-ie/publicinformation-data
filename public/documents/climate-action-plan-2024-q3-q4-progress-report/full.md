### 1 Introduction

*Pages 3–3*

#### This Progress Report on the Climate Action Plan (CAP) 2024 details progress on the 92

#### new actions that were due for completion in Q3 and Q4 2024 and the 13 actions

#### delayed from Q1 and Q2 2024. In total, 53 of the 92 new Q3 and Q4 actions have been

#### delivered on time, alongside 7 of the 13 previously delayed Q1 and Q2 actions.

#### Combining activity across the quarters, this gives an overall implementation rate of 64%

#### for CAP24 with 80 of 125 actions completed over the year.

#### As with the last progress report, high impact actions delayed from CAP23 are also

#### included in this report to retain accountability to their completion. Of the 26 legacy

#### CAP23 actions remaining, 8 of these were completed by the end of Q4 2024. Given

#### their status as high impact actions, remaining delayed actions from CAP23 and CAP24

#### will carry forward for delivery and reporting with new actions from CAP25.

#### Delivery results in 2024 highlight difficulties with high impact actions continuing to

#### experience delays, some for over a year. Resolution pathways are required to overcome

#### challenges to implementation. Otherwise, implications arise for the accuracy of the EPA’s

#### emissions projections and, in turn, the ability of the system to successfully plan and

#### implement policies to sufficiently reduce greenhouse gases (GHGs) in keeping with

#### legislated targets. Failure to implement committed actions further contributes to

#### reputational and litigation risks for the State and exposes it to mounting costs of

#### compliance with exacting EU targets.

#### To provide more detail on these risks and delivery profiles, this report is organised into

#### sectoral chapters in order of emissions impact, alongside a chapter on cross-cutting

#### activity. Throughout the chapters, attention is given to Key Performance Indicators

#### (KPIs), national emissions trends and projections1, detail from the 2024 State of the

#### Environment report by the EPA, and high impact action case studies.

#### Information on every reportable action from the CAP24 Annex of Actions is contained at

#### Appendix 1.

### 1.2.2 Overcoming delays to climate action delivery

*Pages 11–12*

In total, 63 actions are reported as delayed at the end of 2024, including 45 actions from CAP24

and 18 remaining legacy high impact actions from CAP23. The most common reasons reported

by Departments for delivery delays, in order of impact, include:

Technical aspects: adding complexity and/or the need for further data or research;

Issues of alignment: whereby action delivery is dependent on other actions or EU/national policy

completing first;

Discrete issues: including slight delays that are due to resolve shortly and more isolated cases of delay

including difficulties in legal drafting, supply chain issues, and/or a change of direction in the action

approach;

Unexpected need for further engagement: be it stakeholder, public or internal consultation/dialogue;

Administrative delays: particularly the final clearance required for actions to complete, including

government approval; and/or

Capacity and Capability constraints: including resourcing, procurement delays arising from a reliance on

external consultants and competing work priorities.

#### Delays in climate action delivery must be urgently overcome to accelerate progress and

#### close gaps towards legally-binding emissions reduction targets under international, EU

#### and national regimes. The inability to identify and complete climate actions now will

#### result in steeper trajectories to bring Irish sectors, society and the economy back on

#### track, with reputational, litigation and cost implications associated with failing to do so.

#### All 63 delayed actions will carry forward for delivery and reporting with new actions

#### from CAP25, retaining commitment to high impact uncompleted actions.

Agriculture – Delayed CAP24 Q2 Action

Did you know? A draft Carbon Farming Framework was due to publish for public consultation

in Q2 2024 under AG/24/26. This action, while delayed, has repositioned its timeline in line

with EU regulation publication. In December, the Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming (CRCF)

Regulation was published, establishing the first-ever EU-wide voluntary framework for

certifying carbon removals, carbon farming and carbon storage in products. The advanced draft

of the principles required for the development of an Irish carbon farming framework will publish

for consultation shortly. Consultation results will feed into the development of a National

Carbon Farming Framework, now anticipated for delivery by Q3 2025.

Why is it needed? With the correct methodologies, a successful carbon farming framework can

enable sustainable farming practices. It could facilitate, measure and ultimately reward farmers

for sequestering CO2 in soils, hedgerows and trees (‘carbon farming’) as well as for providing

other natural benefits such as air and water filtration, biodiversity and flood protection, and

climate regulation (‘ecosystem services’). The EU anticipate that carbon farming has the potential

to remove several hundred million tonnes of CO2 per annum by 2030 which will be critical to

achieving climate neutrality across Member States. Finalising a policy position on what carbon

farming could look like in Ireland is importantto progress to its implementation in practice.

Agriculture – Delayed CAP24 Q3 Action

Did you know? Under AG/24/10, DAFM were due to develop a detailed implementation plan

to translate the Teagasc 2023 Marginal Abatement Cost Curve (MACC) and incorporate new

actions into the Climate Action Plan. While some new actions from the MACC are drafted for

inclusion in CAP25, DECC did not publish this document by the end of Q4 due to the change in

Government. Provisional changes to the agricultural inventory in the EPA emissions projections

of July 2024 have also resulted in the need for additional work to complete this action. The

output of this will be incorporated into an updated emissions reduction pathway for the

agriculture sector, and will be incorporated into CAP26.

Why is it needed? A MACC visualises the GHG reduction potential of different mitigation

measures, and the relative costs associated with implementing each. The graph can help

policymakers make informed decisions about how to allocate resources for emissions

reductions, including the least costly ways to achieve targets. Teagasc published an updated

MACC in July 2023, with results highlighting the importance of maximising and accelerating

the adoption rate of known GHG mitigation measures including the age of cattle finishing, feed

additives, diversification, and fertiliser use/type. DAFM will continue to translate this best

available science into policies, actions and practical schemes for farmers to adopt these

necessary measures at pace.

deck battery electric buses entering passenger service by the end of Q4 2024.

What’s new? All 120 double-deck electric buses from the first procurement order entered

passenger service by the end of 2024. As planned, 100 of these buses are in Dublin and the

remaining 20 are in Limerick. The number of electric buses in passenger service is expected to

increase during 2025 as more of the teething issues identified with buses and charging

infrastructure are resolved. A total of 136 high-power charging guns are now also available at

Dublin Bus depots in Dublin. Combined, these activities serve to cut reliance on fossil fuels,

reducing emissions from the transport sector. Looking to the future, the National Transport

Authority intends that 85% of the Dublin Metropolitan Area urban bus network will be operated

by low and zero emission buses by 2032, and solely by zero emission buses by 2035.

Transport – Delayed CAP24 Q3 Action

Did you know? Both the Sustainable Mobility Policy (SMP) and CAP24 include actions to expand

the availability of shared mobility options in Ireland that could lessen reliance on individualised

transport ownership and modes. To progress this objective, the development and publication of

a Policy Statement on Mobility Hubs was included as a Q3 Action (TR/24/17 TF) under CAP24,

which has not completed yet. Combined, the actions commit the Department of Transport to

working closely with shared mobility services providers and partnerships to develop more

coherent policy and supports - including technical innovation and appropriate regulation - to

scale up shared mobility services around the country.

Why is it needed? Shared mobility policies can reduce individual car-dependency, curb related

emissions and increase well-being. Mobility Hubs incorporate a variety of shared mobility

solutions including micro-modes, bikes, e-bikes, cargo bikes and e-cars for shared use, with plans

to be delivered at scale across the five cities in Ireland. Following a public consultation, a draft

of the Policy Statement was completed in 2024, and will be ready for consideration by the new

Government in Q1 2025. Subject to approval, it is now hoped to publish the final Statement by

Q2 2025.

## 9 Conclusion

### 9 Conclusion

*Pages 35–37*

![Figure from page 35](assets/p035-f01.webp)

*Figure from page 35*

To reap the economic advantages of the transition to a climate neutral society, reduce significant

compliance costs for missing EU targets and ensure that Ireland plays its full part in global efforts

in limiting climate change, all CAP24 actions, and delayed high impact CAP23 actions, must be

delivered. Delivery of committed actions, as well as identification of new ones, is essential to

bridge known gaps towards meeting Ireland’s national, EU and international commitments.

The EPA’s State of the Environment report, released in October 2024 and detailed throughout

this Progress Report, paints a picture of overall environmental and climate performance in

Ireland. It is published every four years and represents a landmark document that should guide

and influence policy development, implementation and decision-making for the years ahead.

Course corrective actions are needed across the climate, water, nature, air quality and waste

arenas, with worrying trends reported by the EPA in all environmental sectors. The importance

of change and intervention in how we use our land is particularly highlighted as a significant

contributor to Irish climate, biodiversity and water quality challenges. It is an area that could yield

multiple co-benefits if appropriate actions were pursued in collaboration with all landowners.

The implementation of established EU and national policies and regulations must improve

according to the EPA, with potential stressed, also by the EPA, for the development of a

comprehensive national policy statement on the environment to ensure its inclusion in all

decision-making.

The EPA further stresses the urgency of system-wide change across agriculture, energy,

transport, and industrial sectors and the need to scale-up investment in related infrastructure.

Business-as-usual approaches and a focus on incremental tweaks or efficiencies will not be

sufficient to put Ireland on a pathway to sustainability, to meet challenging environmental and

climate commitments or to safeguard Ireland’s environment, nature, water quality and climate

for the good of current and future generations. Transformative change is required.

Such transformation, the State of the Environment report concludes, will bring the most positive

and worthwhile benefit – the protection of the health of Irish people. The interlinkages between

environment and health are often understated and less understood, with the EPA stressing the

need to reduce our exposure to harmful substances (e.g. radon, air, noise and water pollution),

adapt to existing and mitigate further climate impacts, and restore our damaged ecosystems.

Such efforts, the EPA concludes, will create healthy places for people to enjoy and mean that

people can be healthier and live for longer.

Legacy actions from CAP23 and CAP24 will carry forward for delivery and reporting with new

actions from CAP25. This legacy process seeks to maintain accountability, commitment and

whole-of-government attention to the delivery of successive CAP actions in the pursuit of

Ireland’s ambitious and legally-binding emissions reduction targets.
