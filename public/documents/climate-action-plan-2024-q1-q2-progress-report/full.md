### 1.2 2Overcoming delays to climate action delivery

*Pages 11–11*

In total, 39 actions are reported as delayed in the first half of 2024, including 13 from CAP24

and 26 legacy high impact actions from CAP23. The most common reasons reported by

Departments for delivery delays include:

Need for further stakeholder engagement, including public consultation or internal consensus building

within working groups, departments and agencies;

Layers of administrative clearance, whereby actions are significantly advanced and are awaiting final

approval or publication (e.g. final drafts have been prepared or schemes are awaiting final launch) and

expect completion in Q3;

Issues of sequencing and alignment, whereby the completion of actions is reliant on the finalisation of

another activity or policy both at national and/or EU levels; and

Capacity and capability constraints, including internal resourcing issues and procurement delays arising

from a reliance on external expertise for climate policy or scheme development; and/or

Unexpected legal or technical complexity, often leading to the need for extra time for additional research,

outreach, legal drafting, and/or the development of new working approaches.

Agriculture - Complete CAP23 Legacy Action

Did You Know? In January 2024, a public consultation was initiated on a draft National

Biomethane Strategy. The consultation gathered input from stakeholders on proposed plans to

establish a biomethane industry in Ireland. The consultation closed in March after receiving 200

submissions. The final version of the National Biomethane Strategy published in May.

What’s new? A National Biomethane Strategy is key to scale up indigenously produced

biomethane to a target of 5.7 TWh per annum by 2030. This is equivalent to c.10% of Ireland’s

current overall gas demand. Biomethane will help to decarbonise this energy source and displace

fossil gas in many hard-to-decarbonise sectors. The contribution that biomethane could make to

gas supply in Ireland could also improve energy security, all the while providing an alternative

income for farmers to produce the inputs required (e.g. grass, slurry). The publication of Ireland’s

first National Biomethane Strategy also supports the CAP24 objective of providing land use

diversification options for livestock farmers.

Agriculture – Discontinued CAP23 Legacy Action

Did You Know? Meeting climate targets for agriculture will require transformational change.

CAP23 included an action to develop a proposal for a voluntary cow reduction or exit scheme

(AG/23/10). This proposal did not complete in the first half of 2024 under legacy CAP23 reporting,

with a decision taken by DAFM to no longer pursue this action.

There is a need to enhance the sustainability of agriculture across food production, climate

mitigation, resilience, and energy production. CAP24 re-commits to mobilising recommendations

arising from the Food Vision sectoral groupings, including to incentivise voluntary livestock

reductions. Livestock farmers will continue to be incentivised with diversification opportunities,

including, in organics, biomethane production, tillage, afforestation, and reducing management

intensity on drained organic soils.

Transport - Complete CAP23 Legacy Action

Did You Know? Under Action TR/23/58, the Department of Transport has prepared a high-

powered electric vehicle (EV) charging strategy. The final strategy published in May 2024 as the

‘National Road EV Charging Network Plan’ and with a focus on charging on en-route, motorway,

primary and secondary roads.The Plan outlines a path to achieve cleaner transportation,

alongside proposals for investment, regulation, and policy instruments.

What’s new? Fast, reliable, and widespread EV charging infrastructure will support the move

away from fossil-fuelled cars. Fleet electrification is expected to provide the greatest share of

emissions abatement in the medium term in the transport sector under CAP24. Ireland also has

significant obligations to meet under the EU Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation, with

mandatory targets set for the accessibility, type and location of charging facilities along our road

networks. A comprehensive National EV Charging Network Plan will help to guide and ensure

this investment.

Transport - Delayed CAP23 Legacy Action

Did You Know? Drafting of a National Transport Demand Strategy (TR/23/19) made significant

progress in the first half of 2024, with a Draft Strategy and Implementation Plan launching for

public consultation in April. Public consultation and targeted stakeholder engagement on the

Strategy will continue until August and an SEA/AA screening process will also be undertaken in

this period. It will then be resubmitted to Government for approval and final publication in Q4.

Why is it needed? The new strategy, entitled ‘Moving Together: A Strategic Approach to Improving

the Efficiency of Ireland’s Transport System’, aims to reduce the economic and social costs of

congestion and car-dependency alongside significant investment and scale-up in public transport,

active travel and EV infrastructure. It is crucial for better transport planning, containing options

that can be taken at national, regional and local levels to help avoid and shift Irish transport

emissions. It will make the country’s transport system work better for everyone.

Electricity – Complete CAP24 Action

Did You Know? Under EL/24/17, the number of large fossil-fuel electricity generators that must

operate on Ireland’s grid has been reduced.EirGrid is now able to operate the grid with just four

large conventional fossil-fuelled generators (down from five) when there is sufficient power

available from renewable generators such as wind and solar. The new operational policy came

into place on 7 April 2024.

What’s new? The reduction in fossil fuel generators in the Republic of Ireland means that the all-

island electricity grid can now operate with seven large conventional fossil-fuelled generators in

total, four in the State (down from five) and three in Northern Ireland (unchanged). This is

important to facilitate higher levels of renewables on the system and reduce carbon emissions

from non-renewable generation that would otherwise use fossil fuels like coal, oil or gas. EirGrid

is aiming to further reduce the minimum requirement for conventional fossil-fuelled generation

over the coming years.

Electricity - Delayed CAP23 Legacy Action

Did You Know? CRU conducted an extensive public consultation in Q1 2024 to support the

development of a National Energy Demand Strategy and Implementation Plan. This is a legacy

action from CAP23 (EL/23/24) and, due to extended consultation timelines, is now expected to

finalise in Q3 2024.

Why is it needed? Reducing and managing the amount of energy consumed is a priority for

meeting carbon budgets. This can be achieved in multiple ways, including through improving

energy efficiencies and enhancing demand flexibilities for consumers. Demand flexibility allows

energy customers to optimise their use of renewable generation by shifting demand to periods

of low carbon intensity generation. The proposed strategy will thus lead to decarbonisation,

enhanced security of supply, and lower system costs, and is critical in the context of a growing

economy. CRU sought out specific, actionable short- and medium-term measures in the

consultation period, and completed the exercise in tandem with a consultation that reviewed

Large Energy Users Connection Policy given their significant impact in this area.

Cross-cutting - Complete CAP24 Action

Did You Know? Government policy is moving Ireland towards achieving a 'circular economy'.

We cannot continue to ‘make, use and throw away’ in linear patterns of production and

consumption. Circularity means using our resources more sustainably and, by moving to a circular

economy, we can reduce material use. To date, over approximately 60% of plastic bottles and

cans are being collected for recycling through recycling bins, which means that the remainder are

not collected, leading to increased littering.

What’s new? Under Action CE/24/3, a new Deposit Return Scheme, overseen by Re-turn, was

launched on 1 February 2024 and incentivises the return of used drinks containers to be recycled

into new ones. By creating a closed loop recycling system, the scheme aims to boost recycling

rates in Ireland, whilst reducing the number of plastic bottles and aluminium cans being littered

and sent to landfill or incineration. Early indications from research by Irish Business Against Litter

show that can and plastic bottle litter has reduced significantly in towns and cities since the

introduction of the “Re-turn” scheme.Discarded cans are reported to have reduced by almost

30% and plastic bottle waste was less prevalent than in any previous survey.

Did You Know? Ireland’s Climate Action and Low Carbon Development (Amendment) Act (2021)

required each Local Authority to prepare a Local Authority Climate Action Plan to meet national

climate targets and develop resilience to the impacts of climate change. Local Authorities are key

drivers in advancing climate policy locally. Under CAP24 Action LG/24/1, Climate Action Plans

have now been adopted in all 31 Local Authorities.

What’s new? Completing local climate plans is an important milestone. Collectively, there are

almost 4,000 practical climate measures to be completed over the next five years. Eachclimate

plan is built on a strong evidence base, including likely climate change risks to their area and

Baseline Emissions Inventories for sectors locally. Each Local Authority is also responsible for

establishing a Decarbonising Zone (DZ). DZs are intended to be the focus for a range of climate

mitigation, adaptation and biodiversity measures and will help to identify projects which will

contribute to the delivery of the national climate objective.

Cross-cutting - Delayed CAP23 Legacy Action

Did You Know? AchievingIreland’s climate targets will require transformational change across

our economy and society. This transition must be fair, and just, and the costs must be shared

equitably. Originally due under CAP23, Action JT/23/1, Establish a Just Transition Commission, has

been subject to delays while a dedicated Taskforce scoped possible roles and structures for the

Commission.Government approval of its recommendations and to establish a Just Transition

Commission was secured in April 2024, with a recruitment campaign for members and a chair

expected shortly. It is expected that the Commission will commence its work in Q3 2024.

Why is it needed? A Just Transition Commission will provide advice to Government for the

development of fair and equitable climate policies. Developing a greener economy will not be

socially inclusive by default, and carefully crafted policies are needed to maximise opportunities

and cushion the cost of the transition for those who need it. People must also be equipped with

the skills to participate in new jobs of our future climate neutral economy, with continuous pre-

emptive workforce development required. For individuals, this may mean re-skilling and lifelong

learning to access and create future benefits.

What you can do for climate action with support from Government

Individuals and communities are at the heart of the green transition and the impact of climate change will be felt at every level. CAP24 aims to further empower individuals and communities so that positive choices can be made for the future that will bring long-term benefits across Ireland.

In 2024, the National Dialogue on Climate Action (NDCA) is radically expanding its programme to support communities and groups across Ireland to scale the work they are doing and support those who are not yet engaged. The NDCA employs insights from all engagements to inform communications and strategies across Government, laying the foundations for a future that is fair, equitable, and accessible to all.

The State further provides supports in a range of areas to help individuals play their part in tackling climate change, including:

Improving energy efficiency in buildings and at home Reducing waste Making the switch to renewable energy Improving forestry on our lands Reducing transport emissions Making business more energy efficient
