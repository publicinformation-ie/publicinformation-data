## 5.0 Road Safety initiatives / Traffic Calming Measures - The Road Safety plan 2021-2030 {#item-5-0}

CC gave a verbal update on road safety initiatives / traffic calming measures. She advised that the National Road Safety Plan will be delivered in 3 stages up to 2030 and that MCC will develop its plan based on the provisions of the National Plan \& local issues. MM clarified that the delays are due to GDPR issues surrounding collision data which is being addressed at Oireachtas level. MM advised that Traffic Calming is a statutory function for each LA to have an individual plan tailored to their specific needs. Technical solutions are based on data / statistics collected. The rationale as to how the Transport Dept comes up with the calming measures can be presented to this committee. PM raised a question in relation to why there is a variance on the use of ramps in villages in neighbouring counties. For example, in Louth most villages have ramps as a calming measure but this is not the case in Meath. AS agreed, in relation to ramps being places at schools. COR asked for a list of schools or locations that may need traffic calming measures. MM advised that this was being looked at under the footpath extension programme. With the permission of the Chair, the presentation listed under Item 12 on the agenda was presented to the Committee.

## 6.0 Active Travel - Active Travel Strategy {#item-6-0}

NW gave a brief explanation in relation to the Active Travel Programme and how works and maintenance of active travel infrastructure are funded. DA queries how the Greater Dublin Area (GDA) Transport Strategy affects Meath as Navan is mentioned in it. Meath’s transport Strategy is contained in Chapter 5 of the County Development Plan (CDP) – Movement Strategy. It contains the vision, policy context, policies, objectives, metrics and other elements of a strategy that you would expect to find, which in turn informs the work programmes and action plans that are formulated by the Transportation Department. NW explained that both the CDP and the Climate Action Plan take the GDA Transport strategy among other plans, strategies, and guidelines into account. A brief discussion ensued.

## 11.0 Presentation on Abandonment of unused public roads in accordance with Section 12 of the Roads {#item-11-0}

Act, 1993 CC gave a presentation on the abandonment of local tertiary unused roads and advised that these roads are unused or are now impassible historical roads that have not been used for many years. Many of the roads identified are now overgrown. CC outlined the statutory process to abandon the road and that the right of way would not be extinguished. PM did not agree with the abandonment of roads and has had issues with this in the past. As the SPC is for Policy decisions PM was asked to discuss his concerns in relation to specific cases with MCC officials separately. DA asked if when roads are abandoned, they could be turned into footpaths, MM said he would revert with an answer. CC reassured the Committee that the roads being looked at are extremely rural. PMC asked If the land was being offered back to the adjoining landowner, and she was advised that land transfer was not being proposed at this point but could be accommodated. The committee agreed in principle to commence the process. DA was agreeable on condition that the right of way was not being extinguished. PM asked that his opposition be noted.

Proposed by : Cllr Amanda Smith (AS)

Seconded by : Cllr Sean Drew (SD)

## 12.0 Presentation on proposed Footpath Extension Programme {#item-12-0}

CC gave a presentation on the proposed footpath extension programme. 138 schools surveyed in Meath, 24 post primary are located in urban areas and of the 114 primary schools, many are rural locations. 30 primary schools spread across the county are without footpaths of which 9 of these locations have been identified as schools that could be linked with footpaths. This presentation is not related to safety at school, it is solely on how a policy decision can be made to look at the 9 school locations that have been identified as candidates worth considering to form part of a footpath extension programme. A detailed design will need to be undertaken for each scheme. Cost estimates were presented with a large amount of cost relating to land acquisition and public lighting. AS \& PM queried specific paths. CC agreed to review these after the meeting. Acceptance of the Footpath extension Programme was agreed by the Committee.

Proposed by: Cllr Paddy Meade

Seconded by: Cllr Amanda Smith
