1Executive Summary

This Progress Report on the Climate Action Plan (CAP) 2025 details progress on the new

actions that were due for completion in Q4 2025. In total, 16 of the 37 new Q4 actions

have been delivered on time (Table 1).

2 of the 17 delayed actions from Q1, Q2 and Q3 were also delivered in Q4 (Table 2).

Overall implementation of CAP25 stands at 46 actions out of 82 completed by year end.

High impact actions delayed from CAP24 and CAP23 are also included in this report. Of

the 43 legacy actions remaining, 5 were completed in Q4 2025 (Table 3).

Delays in climate action delivery in Q4 are attributed to several factors including: time

required for legislative responses; delays arising from public consultation; and

dependencies on other actions or policies. These delays threaten Ireland’s ability to meet

binding emissions targets and risk increasing future pressures, costs, environmental

damage and reputational harm. While delivery results in 2025 show that a lot has been

achieved, we need to increase efforts if Ireland is to close the emissions gap.

All delayed and legacy actions will continue to be reported upon into 2026, maintaining

visibility as to their delivery.

To provide more detail on progress, risks and delivery profiles, this report is organised

into sectoral chapters in order of emissions share, with a further chapter on cross-cutting

activity. As in previous progress reports, attention is given to Key Performance Indicators

(KPIs), national emissions trends and projections.

Information on each reportable action from the CAP25 Annex of Actions is contained at

Appendix 1.

To keep up to date with the Government’s Climate Action Initiatives, subscribe to the

monthly Climate Action Newsletter mailing list here.

2Agriculture

Agriculture was responsible for 36.2% of national emissions in 2024, making it the

largest GHG emitting sector in Ireland. There are additional agriculture-related emissions

from grasslands used for farming, which are accounted for within LULUCF emissions. In

2024, agricultural emissions were 20.45 MtCO2eq, a decrease of 1.3% on 2023 levels.

This decrease was driven in large by reductions in overall cattle numbers. In 2024,

methane (CH4) emissions contributed 71.2% to the Agriculture sector, originating from

enteric fermentation, manure management and fuel combustion.

Delayed CAP25 Action TR/25/15 Roll out of key elements of electric vehicle (EV) Infrastructure Strategy

While all schemes under this Action have been launched, the roll out of key elements of the EV Infrastructure Strategy has been delayed in 2025. Significant work in this area has taken

place. For example:

e An initial batch of Light Duty Vehicle (LDV1) sites have been connected to the grid (8 chargers) and were operational before the end of 2025 and work is now continuing to move the LDV1 charging programmes into full delivery. Most of these sites were impacted by a lack of grid capacity. It is expected that the remaining sites will have access to the grid in H1 2026.

These initiatives will significantly expand high-power charging availability across the national, regional and local road network, creating a more convenient and reliable system for drivers nationwide. The LDV2 scheme was awarded in 2025, and several locations were energised in 2025, as per CAP24 legacy action TR/24/22.

Local Authorities are continuing to develop their EV Charging Infrastructure Strategies and will commence implementation planning for projects arising from these strategies in 2026.

Zero Emission Vehicles Ireland (ZEVI) has developed a Local Authority EV Charging infrastructure pilot programme, aiming to develop knowledge and explore innovative solutions for EV charging infrastructure across Local Authorities. Work will continue regarding the roll out of 26 pilot projects across 15 Local Authorities. These pilots include new innovations in renewables, battery backup, behind the meter solutions and flexible payments such as discounted off-peak Neighbourhood Charging. The roll out of these pilots will complete in Q4 2026 and follow the delivery of the first pilot in Westside, Galway City earlier in the year. All infrastructure implementation planning will commence in 2026 and thereafter will be multi-annual as move to delivery in 2027.

Overall, there has been steady growth in the number of EV registrations in Ireland. In October 2025, the Minister for Transport announced that Ireland had already met its CAP target of 195,000 EVs on the road by year-end, a key milestone in the nation’s transition to cleaner transport. By the end of Q4 2025, more than 200,000 EVs had been registered. The EV

Infrastructure Strategy will continue to be critical to keeping pace with this continued growth.

4 Electricity

Energy industries (primarily electricity generation) accounted for 12.3% of national

emissions in 2024. Electricity emissions decreased for a third consecutive year in 2024

and are now at an all-time low across the 1990 to 2024 time series at 7.2 Mt CO2eq.

Reductions are in part due to the increased share of net electricity imports as well as

increased renewable generation. In 2024, renewables accounted for c.40% of electricity

demand in Ireland.

The Forestry Programme for the period 2023-2027 aims to encourage a substantial increase in afforestation by offering attractive and diverse options for planting to support our national target of 8,000 hectares of new forests per year. It also provides for a range of schemes to support the maintenance and sustainable management of existing forests.

In 2025, Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine (DAFM) has undertaken a Mid-Term Review (MTR) of the Forestry Programme in conjunction with members of the Forest Strategy Consultative Committee (FSCC), which represents a broad range of Forestry stakeholders. The existing measures and schemes were reviewed with a view to improving their performance for the remainder of the Forestry Programme 2023-2027 term, to help fulfil our existing forestry policy objectives. The European Commission have been notified of proposed changes.

The proposed outcomes from this Mid-Term review are required to be screened, to determine if they would have any significant effects on the environment. DAFM has accordingly issued a notice of screening to the prescribed statutory authorities.

In addition, following Storms Eowyn and Darragh, significant damage was caused to both Coillte and privately owned forests. This has necessitated substantial re-planning of forest operations over the short and medium in the forest sector as it aims to recover the windblown timber. DAFM has established a Forest Windblow Taskforce which comprises of stakeholders representing forest owners, forestry companies, Teagasc and Coillte. It is meeting regularly to work collaboratively and is focusing on providing practical solutions immediately to those landowners whose forests have been adversely affected and are faced with a major clean-up operation in dealing with fallen trees.

Satellite imagery assessments are indicating that just over 26,000 hectares of forestry have been damaged by windblow, including approximately 13,000 in private ownership. Government approval has now been received to launch a new €55 million Reconstitution Scheme for qualifying private forest owners. The Scheme will provide support to private forest owners for replanting their damaged forest areas. This Scheme is in alignment with the Forestry Programme 2023-2027, which contains a general Reconstitution measure that aims to restore and retain forest land and forest ecosystems following significant damage events. Development of this Scheme is underway, and it is expected that applications for the scheme will open in early 2026.

8Cross-cutting actions

Cross-cutting actions support the delivery of sectoral climate action through improvements in

governance, finance, planning, research, citizen engagement, circular economy, adaptation, and

public sector leadership. Several Departments and Agencies contribute to the completion of

such actions, providing a whole-of-Government response and enabling framework for national

climate action. Information on all cross-cutting actions due for reporting is at Appendix 1.

18 new Cross-Cutting Actions were due for completion in Q4. Of these 9 were completed on

time, including key Actions such as Provide business supports to Small and Medium-sized Enterprises

(SMEs) in the tourism outdoor and recreation sector and Develop ‘green budgeting’ analysis of

expenditure allocations to align with performance reporting.

As featured in the CAP25 Q2 & Q3 Progress Report, the Sectoral Adaptation Plans (SAPs) were

approved by Government in Q4 at a Government Meeting on 7 November 2025. The 13 sectors

are grouped according to the themes of Natural Environment, Built Environment and

Infrastructure, Human, and Economy. Further information on the SAPs is available here.

4 delayed CAP25 Cross-Cutting Actions were also due for reporting in Q4, of which 0 were

completed. These actions included; Publish SEAI’s Energy Research Strategy (under the Research

and Innovation Chapter) and Development of a National Exploration Programme for Critical Raw

Materials (under the Circular Economy Chapter).

2 out of 16 outstanding Cross-Cutting Actions from CAP23 and CAP24 were completed in Q4.

One such completed Action under the Adaptation Chapter was to Improve the resilience of

Ireland’s water infrastructure through implementation of a Nature Based Solutions (NBS) Programme.

Completion of this Action led to the planting of 53,925 native trees over 2024 and 2025,

surpassing the target of 50,000 native trees. Uisce Éireann has completed 6 Drainage Area Plans

and installed 3 Sludge Drying Reed Beds (SDRB’s) as per this Action’s output.

Key legacy Actions that remain outstanding include Develop MPPS and Marine Planning Guidelines;

Develop comprehensive legislation for the identification, designation and management of Marine

Protected Areas and Unallocated savings allocated across sectoral emissions ceilings and approved by

Government.
