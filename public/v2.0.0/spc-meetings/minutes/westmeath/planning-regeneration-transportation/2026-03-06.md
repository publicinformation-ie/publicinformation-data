## Item 1: Minutes {#item-1}

## Item 2: Matters Arising {#item-2}

Jean Ryan, Director of Service, responded to a question regarding the proposed Westmeath County Council Taking in Charge Policy and green space ownership in private developments, advising that the green spaces are included in the entire TIC process to ensure good quality public amenity areas are provided within developments. The possibility of future developments on the green spaces would be considered low risk and would be subject to a Part VIII public consultation including the Elected Members review. Cathaldus Hartin, Senior Planner also advised that the council takes ownership off the green space areas so that they can formally maintain them and those green spaces are zoned as open areas in the Local Area Plan. The Council cannot contravene its own plans and zoning objectives relating to those estates without prior public consultation.

## Item 3: Speed Limit Review Phase 2 - 30kph {#item-3}

Alan Kelly (AK), Senior Executive Engineer, Transport Department, provided a comprehensive update on the next phase in the speed limit review, called the Speed Limit Review 2023, an action arising from the government's Road Safety Strategy 2021 -2030 our journey towards Vision Zero. AK provided details on the current phase, phase two of four phases, which deals with the implementation of the built up and urban area speed limit. This second phase of speed limit changes is to be implemented by local authorities through the adoption of special speed limit bylaws and therefore, where it has proposed to change the existing speed limits, it will be necessary to undertake a statutory public consultation process. It is noted that Local Authorities must ensure the implementation of all urban speed limit changes by the 31st of March 2027, any changes and associated signage infrastructure must be installed at that stage, and prior to that, the by law adoption must be completed by the 30th of October 2026. Members welcomed the update and supplementary questions in relation to timelines for consultations, speed limit reviews and locations, were responded to.

## Item 4: National Planning Framework Implementation: Housing Growth Requirements - {#item-4}

Westmeath County Development Plan 2021-2027 Cathaldus Hartin (CH), Senior Planner, provided a brief update on Westmeath County Council's position in terms of the NPF housing growth requirements. It was noted that WCC were above previous targets and moving forward, WCC have identified that there is scope to provide an additional 35 hectares across the county in line with the NPF housing growth requirements, over a phased basis up to 2034. First was the recent adoption of the Mullingar Settlement Area Plan, then moving onto the Athlone Joint Settlement Plan and then onto the remainder of the county settlements in terms of additional capacity for housing provision up to 2034. Members welcomed the update and CH responded to questions, confirming that Census 2022 was referred to in the review but housing unit figures were prominent in the review and future projections. CH noted that WCC are in the advanced stages of a draft plan for Athlone and will then, towards the end of the year, prepare the third phase of the variation process to include the remaining settlements.

## Item 6: Plan and aims for 2026: {#item-6}

Westmeath County Council Best Practice Tree Management Policy Jean Ryan (JR), Director of Service provides update on the progress of a report on trees in the county, conducted by the Environment Department in conjunction with UCD and once completed it is proposed to invite a representative from the Environment Department to present the report to this SPC committee. In line with this report, it is proposed to conduct a review of Westmeath County Councils current Tree Management Policy at the next SPC meeting with a view to focusing on a county wide remit and examining ownership responsibilities. JR responded to and noted questions and comments regarding tree husbandry, tree planting schemes, public consultation and trees in residential estates across the county by advising committee members that all the above points can be addressed during the review process. Roadside Memorial Policy Colm Smith, Administrative Officer, Transportation notified the committee that a Roadside Memorial policy document was recently circulated to all local authorities by the Department of Transport and Transport Infrastructure Ireland. Westmeath County Council is now required to update its existing Roadside Memorial Policy to reflect the Departments policy. The consultation and review will take place over the coming months and will be circulated through this SPC with a view to present to the committee for review at the next SPC meeting. - Community garden / allotment policy for the county Jean Ryan (JR), Director of Service advises the committee that on foot of a request from the Municipal District, a commitment to review the existing policy could take place at the September SPC meeting. Cathaldus Hartin (CH), Senior Planner notes that the provision of the policy framework would be the responsibility of the Planning Department and the delivery ofthe allotments would require a wider discussion with the Municipal Districts. Jean Ryan (JR), Director of Service suggests that the existing guidelines are to be circulated for the next SPC and that the review could take place at the September SPC meeting.
