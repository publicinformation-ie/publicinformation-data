## Item 5 — Update on EV Charging Infrastructure Strategy {#item-5}

Celina Barrett, A/Director of Services gave a briefing on the EV charging Infrastructure Strategy: She advised the Committee that the climate action plan which maps out the actions the country will take to achieve a 51% reduction in overall greenhouse gas emissions by 2030. EU Fit for 55: Aims to reduce net EU greenhouse gas emissions by at least 55% by 2030. Zero Emissions Vehicles Ireland (ZEVI): has been a €100 million investment in EV infrastructure and part of the EV strategy. The target is 195,000 electric vehicles registered by 2025.The EV Infrastructure Charging Strategy aims to deliver an accessible and user-friendly charging network. Home charging will remain the primary source of charging for EV owners and this still remains a challenge for apartments and homes with private driveways. Private Wires Legislation prohibits the running of cables from homes across to external chargers. There was a recent public consultation on this legislation and ZEVI made a submission requesting the exclusion of this use of cables from the remit of the legislation and we are awaiting the outcome. Ms. Barrett highlighted the various types of charging points: - Destination charging is the facility charge when participating in some other activity, \|.e. sports events, shopping. - En-route Charging is charging adjacent to the road network, normally on the forecourt of a petrol station. ## item 6 -Proposed Roadside Memorial Policy {#item-6}

Mr M. McLoughlin, Senior Executive Officer gave a briefing in relation to Roadside Memorials. He said there is a long tradition of the placement of memorials to remember loved ones who has died in road accidents. This policy was previously brought before the SPC in 2011 and 2016 with no changes were recommended. This is a highly sensitive issue; however, the safety of all road users’ needs to be considered alongside the desire for grieving families to commemorate their loved ones. Some issues to consider include: e Memorials causing a distraction to motorists. e The placement of memorials can cause a safety issue. e Insurance implications in the event of further accidents. e Interference with road maintenance. Currently, the Road Traffic Act has no provisions for memorials. Mr. McLoughlin gave examples of other County Council policies in relation to roadside memorials. The options available to Kildare County Council are: e Removal of all memorials by a specific date. e Remain as is with no enforcement. e Revise the policy with restrictions on scale and size. The following was discussed and agreed: The members of the committee agreed that it would be appropriate to put some guidelines in place should a member of the public request to erect a memorial. It needs to be decided if this should be a policy or guidelines. If this is to be a policy, it will need to go to full council for approval, however guidelines do not. It was agreed by the Committee to revert back to the next meeting with draft guidelines for consideration.
