## 4.0 Road Safety Plan 2025 - 2027 {#item-4-0}

FB informed the Committee members of MCC’s Road Safety Plan 2025-2027. The wait in finalising the plan was due to lack of access to road collision data for GDPR reasons. In accordance with the Governments Road Safety Strategy 2021 – 2030 Meath County Council has been working towards the strategy’s primary objective of reducing the number of road fatalities and serious injuries on our roads by 50% by 2030. This Road Safety Plan will align with the Safe System approach. The first ‘Learn to Cycle’ track opened in Blackwater Park in Navan. The purpose-built facility will now give young children the opportunity to learn to cycle on a track designed to replicate a real roadway, with lines, signs, and pedestrian crossings in a safe environment. This Road Safety Plan was presented to the Road Safety Working Together Group on Tuesday the 18th of November 2025 and will be presented for adopting by the full Pleanary at the next meeting. The Road Safety Plan will be continuously reviewed and all learnings from the actions captured will be communicated to this group and the wider community.

## 5.0 Notice of Motion referred from Pleanary to SPC for discussion {#item-5-0}

“To introduce an immediate ban on the use of weed killer containing glyphosate for the year 2025 by Meath County council and all local authority contractors with a review in Nov/Dec 2025 to assess if it is feasible for the council to introduce a permanent ban.” Submitted by Councillor Alan Tobin \& Councillor Helen Meyer FB informed the Committee that Meath County Council maintains full and ongoing compliance with all EU regulations governing pesticide use. All staff who use pesticides are professionally trained, certified, and registered with the Department of Agriculture, ensuring the highest standards of environmental protection and operational safety. The Council strictly adheres to statutory requirements regarding the storage, handling, and controlled use of all pesticide products. Importantly, the council also complies fully with specific restrictions on pesticide use in sensitive areas, including playgrounds, public amenity spaces, and zones adjacent to watercourses and drainage systems, ensuring that no applications take place where they could pose a risk to public health or the environment. The Council reiterated its ongoing commitment to reducing pesticide usage wherever possible and continues to introduce and expand environmentally friendly weed-control methods, such as mechanical removal, alternative lower-impact products, and innovative techniques like hot-foam treatments. These approaches highlight the Council’s environmental leadership, though they are more labour-intensive and often result in quicker weed regrowth compared to glyphosate-based treatments. It was also acknowledged that in certain circumstances—particularly the treatment of invasive species such as Japanese Knotweed—glyphosate remains the most effective and environmentally responsible tool available, preventing the need for more disruptive or resource-heavy interventions. Following discussion, the Committee recognised the merit of the Motion while also acknowledging the practical realities involved. Members agreed that an immediate ban would not be feasible at this time, given current operational needs and regulatory best practices. The Committee commended the Council’s ongoing efforts to minimise pesticide use and actively pursue sustainable, eco-friendly alternatives.

## AOB {#aob}

MB would like to acknowledge the departure of Caroline Corrigan, Senior Engineer, and to thank her for her contribution to the Transportation Urban Regeneration SPC. MB welcomed the LDR4 Abbeylands, Navan scheme but noted some knock-on effects. He requested a similar update that was given at the Municipal District meeting. MM advised that traffic volumes on the Navan Ring Road have decreased by 19%, with a further 16% reduction recorded on the Ratholdren Road. These reductions have improved overall mobility around the town. The Transport Department continues to actively monitor traffic conditions and make ongoing adjustments to signalisation. MM noted that the Transport Department will meet with Blackwater Retail Park businesses early next week to consider revisions to the signalised junction for the Christmas period. The junction is now operating safely. While a significant volume of traffic continues to arrive from the Kells Road, an alternative route is available to road users. PMC stated that positive feedback is being received and highlighted the modification to the cycle of the junction lights. PM noted that there is no legislation regarding the topping of hedges and inquired whether the council holds any statutory powers on this matter. This issue is particularly relevant where overgrown hedges can impede thawing on icy mornings. FB moted that the shelter provided by roadside trees and vegetation can have beneficial effects in the warmer weather to protect the road surfaces from extreme temperatures.
