## Item 4 — Update on the Urban Speed Limit Review Process {#item-4}

Mr. Jonathan Hennessy, Senior Executive Engineer, Sustainable Transport and Traffic Management gave a detailed presentation outlining Kildare County Council Urban Speed Limit Review process as follows: Background: \> \|In 2023 a speed limit review was completed by the Department of Transport. \>» Based on a direction received from the Department of Transport the following speed limits are to be implemented. - 60km/h (Rural Local Roads) -30kn/h (Urban Areas) to be implemented by 31 March 2027. - 80km/h (National Secondary Roads) implementation date to be confirmed. \>» The new speed limits are to take effect on a staggered basis with provisional commencement date as follows -31 March 2027 for Urban Areas. Background to the Implementation of new 30kph Urban Area Speed Limit: \> Safe Systems Approach \>» Policy Background \> Safe Speeds \> Principle of Self Enforcement (30 km/h \> New Steps \> Draft Urban Area Examples In 2019, the European Commission introduced new EU road safety measures for 2021-2030, aiming to halve road deaths and serious injuries by 2030. The 3 Principles agreed: \> Human beings make mistakes » Human body has a limited physical ability. \>» Shared responsibility The Policy background is based on the following: » Programme for Government \> National Strategies \> EU /International Directives. The overview of Government Road Safety Strategy aims to cut road deaths and serious injuries in Ireland by 50% by 2030 under the following headings: e Safe roads and roadsides e Safe speeds e Safe vehicles e Safe road use e Post-crash response e Safe and healthy modes of travel e Safe work-related road use The implementation of the new 30km/h Urban Area Speed Limit principle of “Self- Enforcement” meaning that the design led compliance rather than reliant on An Garda Siochana, to be implemented only in areas of high pedestrian and cyclist demand and use only in areas where 30km/h will realistically be adhered to but to name a few. Councillor Paul Ward requested clarification on self-enforcement and the public perception of the review. Mr Hennessy advised that the concept is for the need of the public behaviour to driving to change. Cllr Tracey O’Dwyer requested clarification on the Design Manual for Urban Roads and Streets (DMURS). Cllr Ger Dunne expressed his concerns on the fatalities on urban and rural roads. Mr Jonathan Hennessy reiterated that DMURS is still at junctions, but no additional funding is available to implement where it will be in urban and residential. He advised that there is a benefit implementing the Urban Speed Limit. Mr. Donal Hodgins informed the SPC committee members that the Urban Speed Limit review is a Reserved Function, and the complete process will remain a challenge. He advised them that Transport Infrastructure Ireland (TII) do not want the 30kph on the National Roads. Mr. Jonathan Hennessy concluded the briefing that the members of the council will be informed of the workshops before going to public consultation. ## Item 6 —Briefing on the Winter Maintenance {#item-6}

Mr. James Malone, Senior Executive Engineer gave the members an update in respect of the Winter Maintenance Plan. The objective of this plan is to keep all the principal roads in the County open to traffic, free from ice and safe to travel on. The season for salting starts mid-October and runs to the end of April with salting runs being carried out twice a night depending on the weather conditions. The presentation outlined the following: \> Road Network- Winter Maintenance \> Current Practice — Routes Covered \>» Demand of Additional Routes » Assessment and Criteria used The New Road Requests for salting are based on the following: » Representations from elected members (Local and National) \> Knowledge of winter accidents » Feeback from Municipal District staff. » New constructed roads like the Athy Distributor Roa (ADR) Following a review on the Council Winter Maintenance Plan 2025/2026 and with the location of the new machinery yard. The salting route increased by 15 km and areas like Kilteel, Sunneyhill and Athy Distributor Road (ADR) are now included in the salting route. The salting route covers 28.5% of the total roads (National Secondary, Regional and Local roads that Kildare County Council covers. The Transport, Infrastructure Ireland (TIl) MmaRC Contractor covers 140km in the County. He informed the members that currently 5 County Councils are collecting salt from Kildare County Council, Machinery Yard. Mr. Malone reiterated the importance to continue to work with Transport Infrastructure Ireland on the operation of the Regional Salt Barn. It is predicted that 5,000 ton is required for the remainder of 2026 and the salting shed has the capacity of 10,000 ton. The Kildare County Council budget allocation excluding salt is €682,500. Mr. James Malone advised the members that the limited scope to increase the 10 routes for salting is maxed, due to Capital /Staff investment needed that would add to annual costs and the working time directives dictate permitted safe driving durations. Cllr Noel Connolly requested clarification on whether the supply of salt is sustainable and the issue with salt banks in housing estates. Cllr Tracey O’Dwyer spoke about the roads taken off the winter maintenance map on the Kildare County Council website. Cllr Ger Dunne commented on the need for footpaths to be salted. In response, Mr. Chris Galvin clarified that Kildare County Council don’t have an ongoing demand for salt banks in estates. He outlined that 10 trucks with service resources are stretched to deliver salt to the routes in line with the Winter Maintenance Map. He reiterated that the main challenge is to reduce fatalities on the roads when carrying out the salt routes during the winter period. He agreed that the members comments on the gritting of the footpaths would be examined and further discussions with the Municipal District Engineers.
