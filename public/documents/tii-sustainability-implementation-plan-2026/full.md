### Foreword from TII’s Chief Executive

I am pleased to present the third iteration of Transport Infrastructure Ireland (TII)’s Sustainability Implementation Plan. As the steward of National Roads, Luas Light Rail, MetroLink and Greenways, TII has a responsibility to protect Ireland’s national transport assets. This means managing and maintaining infrastructure so that it delivers long-term value, meeting the needs of current users while safeguarding it for future generations.

This Plan sets out how TII is embedding sustainability across our major portfolio of national infrastructure programmes, from planning and design through to delivery, operation and maintenance. It reflects the progress we have made since the first Sustainability Implementation Plan was published in 2021, and how its principles are now integrated across the standards, processes and decision-making at the core of our work. This revised Plan builds on that progress and sets out our approach for the next phase of delivery.

We have made good progress to date, but there is more to do. We look forward to continuing this work in partnership across the transport sector, as we deliver a more sustainable transport system that supports quality of life, balanced growth and respects the environment.

This Plan has also been updated to be more focused and accessible, enabling a clearer understanding of TII’s sustainability approach across a wider audience. It places greater emphasis on social value and the outcomes we deliver for communities, while providing a more direct link between long-term policy commitments and practical delivery across TII.

Responding to climate change is central to TII’s purpose, vision and values. We will continue to strengthen our approach to both mitigation and adaptation, reducing the whole-life carbon impact of our infrastructure and services while building resilience to future climate risks.

This reflects both the scale of the challenge ahead and the evolving role of transport infrastructure in enabling access to employment, housing, education and healthcare. It sets out how TII will respond to these challenges, supporting the development of climate‑resilient, low‑carbon transport networks and services that are safe, reliable, and accessible, delivering long-term value for Ireland.

Chief Executive, Transport

### Executive Summary Our Sustainability Implementation Plan

Transport Infrastructure Ireland (TII)’s remit is to secure the provision, development and safe operation of Ireland’s National Road Network, light rail and metro infrastructure, and greenways. TII’s Sustainability Implementation Plan (SIP) sets out our six core sustainability principles as a key component, supporting TII’s vision (see Figure 1). The SIP principles underpin TII’s sustainability efforts and are aligned with the United Nations (UN) Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

The SIP provides the practical framework through which TII gives effect to its Statement of Strategy 2026–2030, translating strategic objectives into measurable, organisation‑wide sustainability actions. The SIP outlines the approach to governance, targets and performance monitoring, and supports the integration of environmental, social and governance considerations across TII’s activities.

Through the establishment of Sustainability Principles, Sustainability Outcomes, and aligned targets and metrics, the SIP enables TII to track progress, demonstrate accountability, and provide assurance to stakeholders, while supporting the delivery of safe, resilient and sustainable transport infrastructure for Ireland. The SIP further aligns our project delivery with current national targets and legislation, ensuring we contribute to achieving national decarbonisation goals, creating sustainable transport and delivering positive social impact.

First published in 2021, subsequent updates in 2024 and 2026 ensure that the SIP remains up to date and fit for purpose. This keeps sustainability relevant and embedded throughout our remit and across the organisation to support the delivery of TII’s national targets, actions and strategic ambitions. This document sets out TII’s progress in delivering the ambitions of the 2021 and 2024 iterations of the SIP, highlighting the tangible steps taken to embed sustainability across the organisation and the outcomes being realised in the planning, delivery and operation of transport infrastructure. It also reflects TII’s ambition to continue strengthening sustainability integration as part of everyday decision making and delivery.

### Background on Sustainability

![Figure 4: Policy hierarchy which influences TII’s organisational policies](assets/p009-f01.webp)

*Figure 4: Policy hierarchy which influences TII’s organisational policies*

The Climate Crisis Climate change remains a global concern, with projections from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) indicating under current trajectories, global temperatures are expected to rise beyond 1.5°C in the early 2030s, crossing the threshold set by the Paris Agreement (2015) to stay below 2°C above pre-industrial levels. Exceeding these thresholds is expected to result in significant and widespread impacts for many regions. In response, the Government of Ireland and numerous other countries have pledged to achieve net-zero emissions by 2050, aiming to protect both our future and that of generations to come.

Policy and Legislative Context TII’s organisational policies and commitments are shaped by global, European and national policy frameworks as illustrated in Figure 4. These frameworks, such as the EU Green Deal, establish binding requirements, shared ambitions and national targets that guide how sustainability is addressed across the transport sector.

The Climate Action and Low Carbon Development (Amendment) Act 2021 facilitates Governmentapproval of plans related to climate change, such as the Climate Action Plan 2025 (CAP25), which requires all public bodies to submit annual updates on the progress of climate change mitigation and adaptation measures. In response to this, TII reports on sustainability within its Annual Report and annual Climate Action Roadmap.

TII Policy Our 2024 Sustainability Statement commits TII to embedding sustainability across the development, operation and management of the National Road, light rail, and greenway networks. This commitment ensures TII delivers safe and resilient infrastructure, creates better accessibility and sustainable mobility for people and goods, enables economic efficiency, and enhances environmental systems for current and future generations. The Statement of Strategy 2026-2030 reaffirms this through TII’s vision: to support the advancement of a connected and sustainable future for Ireland through a safe, efficient and integrated transport network. Sustainability underpins the Statement of Strategy. As a public body, TII is directed and governed by external stakeholders such as the Department of Transport and is required to consider the wider national and international sustainability context that informs and shapes its approach to project delivery.

The SIP enables alignment with this Act to deliver and report on sustainable activities, demonstrating how TII embeds sustainability throughout its projects and activities. Regular reviews of the SIP also ensure that TII remains cognisant of updates to climate guidance and legislation, ensuring alignment with national and international legislation and continuing to contribute to national climate objectives. The SIP provides the framework to ensure sustainability is integrated into all of the work that TII undertakes. This ensures that delivery across the organisation is consistent with internal expectations while supporting the achievement of national policies, objectives and wider societal outcomes. A full policy review to support this iteration of the SIP is provided in Appendix A.

### Measuring Progress

TII has made significant progress in delivering on the original SIP. Details are set out in Appendix D and E. Progress is currently assessed qualitatively but where possible TII is capturing quantitative metrics. TII has created management processes, engagement platforms and reporting lines that have resulted in a higher level of awareness and accountability for delivery progress across the organisation.

Sustainability Outcomes

SIP review

Enhancing Reporting

Through the establishment of a clear Sustainability Outcomes Framework, TII has put in place a structured approach for translating national policy objectives into measurable, organisation wide outcomes. These outcomes support prioritisation of key sustainability activities, government commitments and guide the development of business activities towards more sustainable deliverables for TII overall. The Sustainability Outcomes for each Sustainability Principle are set out in Appendix B and illustrate the progress TII is making towards applying the Sustainability Principles in all of our work.

The SIP will be reviewed on a periodic basis as appropriate. This will enable us to benchmark our progress, realign our ambition and determine if there are new opportunities to take that will accelerate our journey to a sustainable future.

Tracking sustainability progress is critical to ensuring TII delivers on the SIP and embeds the six Sustainability Principles in all its activities. Regular meetings of, and reporting to, the Assurance Board, have been supplemented with annual Highlights Reports, quarterly reports on the Sustainability Outcomes, contributions to the TII Annual Report and an annual Climate Action Roadmap all capturing the tangible progress achieved.

Metrics

Existing TII KPIs are used to measure TII’s progress towards delivering each of its key national targets, as set out in each of the Sustainability Outcomes tables found in Appendix B. These metrics are used to support the internal tracking of TII’s strategic objectives. Building on this foundation, further development of KPI based measurement will enable TII to monitor progress, ensure data quality and accountability, and support evidencebased decision making across its programmes and projects. Environment The following policies are shaping the environmental performance of the transport sector by driving decarbonisation, advancing circular economy principles, improving energy efficiency, and promoting the protection and enhancement of biodiversity.

The revision of the EU Energy Efficiency Directive (2023) significantly raised EU ambitious targets on energy efficiency, giving it legal-standing for the first time, meaning energy efficiency must be considered by EU member states in policy development and investments put forward in the energy and non-energy sectors. The EU Renewable Energy Directive aims to increase renewable energy in the EU energy mix, raising the target to 42.5% for 2030, compared to the previous target of 32%.

The EU Action Plan: ‘Towards Zero Pollution for Air, Water and Soil’ to 2050 (2021) has a zeropollution vision for 2050 for air, water and soil pollution to be reduced to levels no longer considered harmful to health and natural ecosystems.

The EU Water Framework Directive (WFD) is the main law for water protection in Europe and ensures an integrated approach to water management, respecting the integrity of whole ecosystems, including by regulating individual pollutants and setting corresponding regulatory standards.

European policies The European Green Deal (2019) recognises the importance of multimodal transport and commits to increasing the efficiency of the transport system and reduce GHG emissions. The European Commission under the EU Green Deal has proposed new CO2 emissions targets for heavy-duty vehicles from 2030 onwards, with all new city buses being zero-emission by 2030, contributing to the EU’s climate and zero pollution objectives, and providing benefits for transport operators and users, aiming to ‘accelerate the shift to sustainable and smart mobility’. The EU’s Fit for 55 is a package of policy proposals that aim to reduce the EU’s greenhouse gas emissions by 55% by 2030, relative to 1990 levels, responding specifically to the second objective of the European Climate Law (55% greenhouse gas emission reduction by 2030). The Fit for 55 package has been adopted and begun implementation across multiple directives. It requires increased emission reduction targets for all Member States across sectors by 2030, which will further impact requirements of emission reductions required by the transport sector.

The EU revised the Ambient Air Quality Directives (2022), including closer alignment to World Health Organisation recommendations, and aims to achieve zero air pollution by 2050.

At EU level, controls on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in water have been significantly strengthened through Directive (EU) 2026/805, which entered into force in May 2026 and updates the Water Framework Directive, Groundwater Directive and Environmental Quality Standards Directive. The Directive introduces groups of PFAS into the EU’s regulated pollutant framework, strengthens monitoring requirements, and provides the basis for future environmental quality standards for groups of PFAS in surface water and groundwater. While the Directive requires transposition into Irish law by December 2027, it signals a clear tightening of regulatory expectations for emissions and runoff management.

The EU Circular Economy Action Plan (2020) outlines a number of key actions to promote the transition of a linear model to one that is circular, keeping materials and resources in use for longer. Construction and buildings need to focus on addressing the sustainability performance of construction products in the context of the revision of the Construction Product Regulations (2024) and introduce recycled content requirements for certain construction products, taking into account their safety and functionality.

The EU Nature Restoration Law (2024) establishes legally binding obligations to restore degraded ecosystems and improve ecological resilience, requiring Member States to implement effective restoration measures to restore 20-30% of degraded ecosystems by 2030, and restore all degraded ecosystems by 2050.

## Governance

### Governance

*Pages 50–50*

TII’s Sustainability Portfolio was established to consolidate and align sustainability activities across divisions. By bringing together diverse sustainability initiatives, the portfolio coordinates delivery of sustainability related outcomes and fosters a collective approach towards achieving TII’s sustainability goals. TII’s Sustainability Portfolio can be defined through three vertical levels of governance: 1) Assurance; 2) Progress, and 3) Steering, which are supported by a centralised Portfolio Management Office (PMO).
