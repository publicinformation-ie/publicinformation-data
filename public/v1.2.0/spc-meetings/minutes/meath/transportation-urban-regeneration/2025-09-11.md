## 3.0 Presentation on Winter Maintenance 2025-2026 {#item-3-0}

CC gave a presentation on Winter Maintenance 2025-2026 giving an overview of the delivery of service which is at full capacity. CC informed the Committee that the council is always learning and improving to deliver the service safely, efficiently and building resilience while reducing risk and limiting the impact on the day to day services. Following the presentation, the members noted the following.

- DA asked if cycle lanes are gritted.

- PM would like to have it recorded that he would like to have more roads included on the gritting route and that looking at the possibility of additional trucks and staff should be considered.

- SD queried regarding the Salt Bin Policy how much would it cost for the Council to fill the salt bins as community groups and schools do find it difficult to cover the cost of the salt.

- SD made reference to driver behavior and suggested that the council could be more proactive promoting winter tyres and reiterate that when roads are gritted there is still a need to reduce speed on treated roads.

- SD asked about the routes gritted within towns. Does the policy refer to how footpaths are treated.

- MB queried is the figure of €21,000 per run, does that include all costs associated with carrying out the run and would like to acknowledge the work done by the staff. CC \& MM responded as follows;

- The gritter machines have settings depending on the area that is being covered. The gritter’s mechanism feeds the salt from the hopper onto a rotating disc which then spins and spreads the salt in a consistent layer across the road where a bouncing effect is created covering cycle lanes and footpaths where applicable.

- 22% of the Roads Network in the County is gritted. It is acknowledged that there is an annual debate about extending the routes.

- The price of salt bins was increased to capture the full cost. There are many full salt bins within the county that have not been used, however MCC can look at providing salt for locations where salt bins are used. There would be cost / budget implications.

- MCC Officials will contact the Communications Department to ensure that there is a Winter Awareness Campaign featured on all social media platforms.

- €21,000 cost per run includes all associated costs, such as machinery, salts, wages etc.

## 4.0 Presentation on the Draft Traffic Calming Policy {#item-4-0}

CC gave a presentation on the Draft Traffic Calming Policy. The policy sets criteria for the assessment and delivery of Traffic Calming measures to reduce the adverse effects that speed and volumes of traffic have on our roads. Traffic Calming aims to improve road safety and enhance the environment for all road users and allows for a programme of schemes to be developed and to seek annual funding.

- Central delivery allows for better management of works, greater efficiency and VFM.

- Road Safety Engineer has key role in co-ordinating and maintaining the register.

- Provides consistency and clarity for stakeholders.

- Allows for a programme of schemes to be developed and to seek annual funding.

- Formal capture of requests for traffic calming.

- All data, information, etc. stored centrally. Following the presentation, the members would be supportive of this policy and requested a two- week timeframe to revert back with any comments or observations. MM informed the members that a decision was not due today but would propose a budget with a view to having the policy adopted at the next plenary. Once adopted we can then devise a programme and bring this through the budgetary process. Having a defined policy will also assist public representatives and stakeholders to manage requests for traffic calming measures in a formal and fair manner, apply for funding efficiently and will allow MCC to put the right traffic calming measures in the right places. SD asked that the decision be deferred to allow time to review and make comments on the policy. DA stated that their members would be delighted to see this structured process and had a question around the baseline road safety statistics. CC advised that our data comes from the RSA and the Gardaí. A data protection issue arose in relation to the sharing of statistics, which should be resolved by the end of the year. MCC expects to get notice from the Minister in relation to urban 30kph speed limits in the coming months. This will be a large body of work. consultation process, public consultation, submissions etc. It was agreed to seek a budget allocation, subject to the SPC returning their comments by 25th September 2025, to allow us to bring the draft Policy to the MDs and the Plenary.

## 5.0 Presentation on Community Centers Footpath Programme {#item-5-0}

CC gave a presentation on Community Centers Footpath Programme and of 120 centers and clubs surveyed 84 have existing footpaths links, 36 with no links, 11 with potential to have links with some of these being unsuitable i.e. position on 80 KPH road with 4 locations identified for the proposed footpath works. The minimum cost of works would be in the region of €200,000 to deliver but this figure does not include potential land acquisition or boundary/verge re-installation. If funding becomes available, acceptance of the Community Centers Footpath Programme was agreed by the members. PM would like it noted that where the proposed road is being done on the Grange to Clontail Scheme – Newtown County Club there is a walkway coming up to 300 meters to the soccer pitch.
