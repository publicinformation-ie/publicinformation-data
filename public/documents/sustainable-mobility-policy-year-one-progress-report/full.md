## Executive Summary

### Executive Summary

*Pages 4–4*

National Sustainable Mobility Policy | Year One Progress Report

The National Sustainable Mobility Policy (SMP) was published in April 2022 with a target of delivering at least 500,000 additional daily active travel and public transport journeys, and a 10% reduction in kilometres driven by fossil-fuelled cars, by 2030. The Climate Action Plan 2023 updated these targets to a 50% increase in daily active travel journeys, a 130% increase in daily public transport journeys, and a 20% reduction in total vehicle kilometres travelled by 2030.

The following highlights some of the key progress achieved during the first year of the SMP:

Ӄ Established a Research Network to help focus resources on research priorities that are critical to providing an evidence base for delivery of key objectives under the SMP.

Ӄ Established a new division - the Sustainable Mobility Management and Implementation Division (SMMID) - within the Department of Transport (DoT) to focus resources on demand management, shared mobility, and delivery of actions under the SMP, including the establishment of a Portfolio Management Office (PMO).

Ӄ Contributed to the development of a Climate Action Public Engagement Strategy.

Ӄ Convened the first annual National Sustainable Mobility Forum to engage with stakeholders on SMP.

This report outlines the progress made in the first year of the SMP, and related Action Plan, and provides a brief overview of the work to be advanced during the remainder of 2023.

Ӄ Established a taskforce, referred to as the SMP Leadership Group (LG), to oversee and drive the implementation of Policy.

Ӄ Established a Delivery Team (DT) to support the LG and to develop and oversee the delivery of a Pathfinder Programme.2

Ӄ Launched a new Pathfinder Programme consisting of 35 sustainable mobility projects, which are aligned to the SMP. Pathfinder branding also developed and deployed.

Ӄ Planned and delivered workshops to assist with the mitigation of identified risks in the implementation of the SMP.

The 91 actions in the SMP Action Plan are grouped under 10 high-level goals, all stemming from the SMP’s three overarching principles:

To govern delivery of the SMP effectively, the Department of Transport has established a new Sustainable Mobility Management and Implementation Division (SMMID). In addition to having responsibility for policy on the emerging areas of shared mobility, demand management, and mobility hubs, SMMID also provides a secretariat to the SMP Leadership Group and Delivery Team which work to oversee and drive implementation of the SMP Action Plan and the SMP Pathfinder Programme, respectively.

Principle 1: Safe and Green Mobility

1. Improve Mobility Safety.

2. Decarbonise Public Transport.

3. Expand availability of sustainable mobility in metropolitan areas.

4. Expand availability of sustainable mobility in regional and rural areas.

5. Encourage people to choose sustainable mobility over the private car.

Principle 2: People-Focused Mobility

1. Take a whole of journey approach to mobility, promoting inclusive access for all.

2. Design infrastructure according to Universal Design Principles and the Hierarchy of Road Users model.

3. Promote sustainable mobility through research and citizen engagement.

Principle 3: Better-Integrated Mobility

1. Better integrate land use and transport planning at all levels.

2. Promote smart and integrated mobility through innovative technologies and development of appropriate regulation.

*Pages 8–8*

A number of delays reflect alignment issues with other policies and strategies, where actions with shared objectives are replicated in the SMP. For example, five Road Safety Strategy-related actions were delayed at end April – namely actions 7, 64, 65, 66, and 91. While it is anticipated that these actions will be completed in Q3, the department, in conjunction with the Road Safety Authority, commenced a process in early 2023 to consolidate and modify all actions under Phase One of the Road Safety Strategy, and it is intended that any recalibration of actions as part of this process, when concluded, will be reflected in any future updates of the SMP.

Transport for Ireland (TFI) Live App

The new TFI Live App was rolled out in February 2023 and combined the previously available Real Time Ireland and Journey Planner apps.8 The new app fulfils SMP Action 84, and brings together real-time information for Bus Éireann, Go Ahead Ireland, Dublin Bus, Luas, and Iarnród Éireann Irish Rail services, as well as providing other tools for planning and managing transport journeys.

Of the 13 actions that were delayed at end April, two have since been completed. Action 89 relating to the introduction of PPTs was completed with the passing of new Road Traffic and Roads Act 2023 and Action 73 (as above) was completed with the publication of the Transport Appraisal Framework. Accordingly, by the end of June, the number of delayed actions had fallen to 12%.

At Risk of Delay

A number of actions have been identified as being at risk of delay despite progress being made. In some cases, this is due to delays arising in dependent actions – for example, Action 90 relates to usage and safety regulations for PPTs which must be submitted to the European Commission for consideration for a minimum 12 weeks. This piece of work was dependent on the successful enactment of the Road Traffic and Roads Act 2023 which occurred in late June. Thus, while the action is now likely to be complete by end 2023, the earlier delay in enacting the relevant legislation has created a minor risk of late delivery for Action 90.

Delayed

While 14% of actions were delayed by the end of the first year, it is anticipated that most of these actions will be completed shortly - during Q3 or Q4 of 2023. In many cases, the delays were reasonable (to achieve the best outcomes) or unavoidable.

For example, Action 68 called for the development and implementation of a public engagement strategy to promote the benefits of sustainable mobility and raise public awareness of sustainable transport options. However, far from failing to advance, this action has now been integrated into a more wide-ranging CAP23 action to produce a Climate Action and Sustainable Mobility Communications Strategy which is due for delivery early in Q3 2023.

In other cases, the ‘At Risk’ status reflects minor delays to some elements of larger programmes which are otherwise progressing on schedule. For example, Action 12 relates to work being undertaken to deliver DART+. While this is progressing well, with DART+ West and Dart + South-West railway order applications successfully submitted, some elements of the programme’s public consultation have required additional time and are ongoing. The action will therefore continue to be proactively monitored by the SMP Leadership Group to ensure overall progress remains on track, while recognising the importance of affording sufficient time to the public consultation process.

An unavoidable delay arose in relation to Action 73. In this case, it was prudent to await the publication of a related Government Circular before moving to publish the revised Transport Appraisal Framework.

National Household Travel Survey

The National Household Travel Survey is designed to provide a comprehensive picture of the daily travel patterns of Irish residents. The SMP (Action 69) commits to publishing this survey on annual basis so that we can track progress across SMP objectives. Details on the NTA’s most recent National Household Travel Survey in 2022 is planned for release later in 2023. This will be complemented by Action 42 which commits to expanding the Walking and Cycling Index to Cork, Galway, Limerick and Waterford to assess infrastructure; travel behaviour; the impact of walking and cycling; and new initiatives in each city.

Some ‘At Risk’ actions (e.g., Actions 72 and 75) may be subject to potential constraints elsewhere. Work has nevertheless begun on such actions and the department will seek to advance them as quickly as possible, working in conjunction with key staff in the Houses of the Oireachtas.

Overall, the SMP Leadership Group and its Delivery Team will continue to monitor actions which are ‘At Risk’ to ensure they are brought on schedule as soon as possible, and to prioritise those actions which will have a direct and tangible impact in shifting journeys from private cars to more sustainable modes of transport.

Ongoing

Several SMP actions relate to areas of work which take place annually or on an ongoing basis, which are monitored via quarterly updates.

Two of these actions relate to programmes of work whose status will not fully materialise until the end of the delivery period in 2025, e.g. Action 62 which relates to an average number of road safety improvement schemes per year over that period. Another – Action 61 - reflects ongoing interdepartmental collaboration, which began in 2022 but which will continue and even expand over the full lifetime of the SMP.

## SMP Leadership Group, Delivery Team, and Pathfinder Programme

Leadership Group

Delivery Team

The SMP Leadership Group (LG) was established in May 2022 with a secretariat provided by the Department of Transport. Membership of the LG is composed of the National Transport Authority (NTA), Transport Infrastructure Ireland (TII), the regional assemblies (SRA, NWRA, and EMRA), City and County Management Association (CCMA), Department of Housing, Local Government, and Heritage (DHLGH), Department of the Environment, Climate and Communications (DECC), Road Safety Authority (RSA), Sustainable Energy Authority of Ireland (SEAI), as well as several Divisions of the Department of Transport.

The SMP included a commitment to agree a mechanism for the delivery of a programme of ‘pathfinder’ projects which would seek to highlight a broad variety of innovative projects with the potential to transform sustainable mobility options in our cities, towns, and rural areas, and to deliver these projects by 2025. It was envisaged in that this programme would bring increased momentum to the delivery of projects at a local level, providing templates for replication and scaling up elsewhere.

The Delivery Team (DT), which consists of representatives from the LG’s own organisations as well as broader representation when required, was tasked with identifying projects for inclusion in a new Pathfinder Programme. Following a request from the Minister for Transport for local authorities to submit their most transformative project ideas, the Delivery Team assessed these against several criteria including overall suitability for the programme in terms of policy alignment, level of ambition, capacity to offer a new approach to addressing sustainable mobility challenges, and ability to deliver by end 2025. A final list of 35 projects was approved by the Minister for the programme in October 2022.

To quickly build the appropriate structures, processes and engagement needed to drive the ongoing implementation of the Policy, the LG met monthly during 2022 and has now transitioned to a quarterly meeting schedule.

During its first six months, the LG contributed to the development of an Implementation Plan for the governance and oversight of SMP delivery, including the establishment of a PMO support function within the Department of Transport. It also considered further work items such as a detailed transport-related input to the National Energy Security Framework, the identification of barriers/obstacles to SMP action delivery, the establishment of a Research Network, and the development of a Climate Action Communications Strategy.

Initially meeting on a weekly basis to develop the Pathfinder Programme, the Delivery Team now meets on a monthly basis to monitor progress on, and mitigate risks to, the delivery of that Programme.

In addition, the LG established a Delivery Team to provide oversight of SMP implementation at operational level as well as to formulate a programme of ‘pathfinder’ projects linked to the SMP and to be delivered by 2025.

The first portion of the Longford Town Core Cycle Network - the N63 Majors Well Road project – has been completed and the full pathfinder remains on track to be delivered by the end of 2024. Two sections of the Cork to Waterford Inter-Urban Demonstrator Pathfinder, namely Bilberry to Waterford City and Dunkettle to Carrigtohill, were completed in June 2023, with further sections due for completion in 2024 and 2025. This pathfinder is helping to create a seamless cycling link between Cork and Waterford.

The SMP Pathfinder Programme was launched on 17 October 2022, consisting of 35 exemplar transport projects to be delivered by local authorities, and other bodies, around the country. Taken together, the proposals offer the potential to transform how we move, work and live by making it easier to leave the car at home and choose a different mode of travel. In addition to showcasing the appetite for change which exists throughout the country, the programme closely aligns with evidence-based best practice for reducing emissions from transport through systemic change.

A key milestone was reached for a Dublin City Centre pathfinder project on 29 May, with the implementation of traffic management measures including a 24/7 bus gate in the College Green/ Dame Street area. This project includes measures that will give more space to pedestrians and improve the efficiency of public transport in the city centre.

The Pathfinder Programme has already seen significant progress, with two projects complete since its initiation. In January, the first full electrification of a town bus service was launched in Athlone, providing clean and green public transport services for the town, and paving the way for a similar transition in other services across Ireland. In May, the National Transport Authority launched a new Smarter Travel Mark. This Mark will provide certification to organisations that support active and sustainable travel and encourage the reduction in single-person car usage amongst their staff, customers, visitors, and students in the case of third level campuses.

All projects are making good progress and are on track for delivery over the lifetime of the Programme (2023-2025).

### Project Management Approach

*Pages 12–12*

Project Management Approach

![Figure from page 12](assets/p012-f01.webp)

Following the establishment of the Leadership Group and Delivery Team, external support was procured to assist with the setting up of a Project Management Office within the Sustainability Mobility Management and Implementation Division (SMMID) of the Department. This contract commenced in September 2022 and SMMID worked intensively with the external contractors to develop the necessary tools to support a PMO-led approach to monitoring the implementation of the SMP.

This encompassed the development of more effective tools for monitoring and reporting on progress in a more granular way, including an Implementation Plan, a PMO Handbook and dashboards to visualise progress in the implementation of the SMP.

Consideration was also given to the identification of high-level risks associated with each of the 91 SMP actions, and a workshop-based approach was instigated to develop potential mitigants to these risks. The PMO team have also applied an ‘Avoid- Shift-Improve’ categorisation to individual actions, aligning with the approach taken in CAP2023.

These tools have provided a structure for the ongoing management of the implementation of the SMP. Towards the end of the first year of the SMP, the department also commenced work on a similar PMO-led approach to the management of the Pathfinder Programme.

## Climate Communications Campaign

### Climate Communications Campaign

*Pages 14–14*

National Sustainable Mobility Policy | Year One Progress Report

There is a need to promote greater awareness and understanding across society about the ambition for transport – and what’s required - in the Climate Action Plan 2023 (CAP23).

There are four core areas underpinning the strategy. These include:

1. Prioritising widespread public communications and engagement; this includes a national advertising campaign due to launch later this summer; this will be supported by various community and stakeholder engagement activities, either through the expansion of existing programmes, or development of new ones

As per Action 68 of the SMP, Government committed to developing and implementing a public engagement strategy to promote the benefits of sustainable mobility and raise public awareness of options. Building on this approach, CAP23 includes a new and enhanced action (TR/23/6), which provides for the development and implementation of a ‘Climate Action and Sustainable Mobility Communications Strategy’.

2. Developing more consistent, proactive consultation and communication techniques to enhance project acceptance rates amongst communities for active travel and public transport infrastructure projects; lessons learned from the delivery of past projects will be incorporated, best-practice case studies will be developed and additional support for robust evaluation methods will be provided, amongst others

A multi-layered communication and engagement strategy is being developed and will be implemented over the period 2023 to 2025. The main objective of the strategy is to build awareness and acceptance, and inspire ownership for action, across individuals, private organisations and public sector bodies, whilst also increasing support for delivery of critical infrastructure. Details of this strategy were also included in a Memorandum for the Information of the Government that was brought to cabinet in April 2023.

3. Exploring the potential for the role of car advertising and labelling to support consumers in making more informed and sustainable choices when purchasing new cars or considering the best mode of transport for different journey types; guidelines, new or enhanced self-regulatory frameworks, and a potential longer-term review of regulations are some of the envisaged outputs

4. Developing an evaluation framework and research support, including a long-term attitudes and behaviour impact research study, to measure changing attitudes towards sustainable mobility as the strategy is implemented; in addition, this will be supported by a robust evaluation framework as well as on-going research for best-practice communications and engagement activities in the area of sustainable mobility.

## Climate Action Plan 2023 and Beyond – SMP Alignment

### Climate Action Plan 2023 and Beyond – SMP Alignment

*Pages 15–15*

National Sustainable Mobility Policy |Year One Progress Report

As set out in CAP23, Government has established six climate delivery taskforces in key sectoral areas. The role of the transport taskforce is fulfilled by the SMP Leadership Group (LG). Of the fifteen transport-related work programmes identified in CAP23’s Annex of Actions, ten fall under the remit of the LG. These programmes are specifically concerned with integrating sustainable mobility into our transport system and encouraging a shift to the use of more sustainable modes - such as walking, cycling and public transport.

As the LG was set up to support the delivery of the SMP, the ten programmes for which it is responsible are:

1. Enhancing Governance & Accelerating Implementation

2. Communications Strategy

3. Enhanced Spatial and Land-use Planning

4. Strategic Transport Planning

5. Roadspace Reallocation

Transport-related actions in the CAP23 Annex of Actions consolidates and integrates actions from multiple departmental policies, such as the National Sustainable Mobility Policy, Road Safety Strategy, Road Haulage Strategy, Renewable Transport Fuels Policy, and ZEVI Implementation Plan and Charging Infrastructure Strategy across 15 ‘headline’ or key work programmes, which are grouped in accordance with an AVOID-SHIFT- IMPROVE framework for guiding greater transport sustainability.

6. Active Travel & Accessibility

7. Major Public Transport Infrastructure

8. Public Transport Services Investment & NTA Connecting Ireland Rural Mobility Programme

9. Smart, Shared and Integrated Mobility

10. Decarbonising Public Transport & School Transport Services

The remaining five work programmes are not under the remit of this taskforce and have different governance and oversight arrangements. Moving forward into the second year of the lifetime of the SMP, the LG will meet on a quarterly basis to oversee its implementation. The PMO which was established in December 2022 will continue to support the LG in its oversight function – helping the Group to focus on key issues related to implementation, such as risk management and mitigation, the rollout of a climate communications campaign, and issues arising in the context of Climate Action Plan targets or other emerging policy imperatives.
