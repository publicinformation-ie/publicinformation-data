## Agenda items

## Item 1: Minutes {#item-1}

On the proposal of Cllr Farrell and seconded by James Bourke, the minutes of the SPC meeting held on the 13 February 2025 were approved. The Chairperson confirmed the meeting would be recorded for minute-taking purposes.

## Item 2: Matters Arising {#item-2}

No matters arising were reported.

## Item 3: Update on Mullingar Local Area Plan {#item-3}

Cathaldus Hartin, Senior Planner, provided a comprehensive presentation on the draft Mullingar Local Area Plan currently being finalized before public display. Cllr Gaffney enquired about considerations for rural areas outside Mullingar's scope and the four core policy documents referenced. Cllr Farrell asked whether recent government directives on land zoning for housing acceleration had been factored into the plan. James Bourke questioned the status of framework area plans and provisions for different housing types. Paul Bruton raised concerns about wastewater treatment capacity for the projected housing units. Cllr lanes asked ahout Irish Water capital projects for infrastructure upgrades. Alison Hough raised concerns about the 20-year planning history of the Eastern and Midlands Regional Water Supply project and its environmental challenges, questioning backup plans if the project doesn't materialize. Cathaldus Hartin responded that there is significant wastewater capacity and Irish Water has been engaged throughout the process with no issues identified to date. A submission has been made to the National Housing Strategy to expedite delivery of the Eastern and Midlands Region Water Supply Project, including the spur to Mullingar and surrounding settlements, as a critical long-term solution to address environmental constraints on the existing Lough Owel supply, ensure sustainable population and housing growth, and enable regional economic development. The Draft Plan also addresses the housing requirements of the town and includes a commitment to cooperate with the relevant landowners, in conjunction with investors and government agencies, in the preparation of Regeneration Plans for both Columb Barracks and attendant lands and St. Loman’s Hospital and attendant grounds, to consider future options with a view to securing viable alternative uses and the creation of sustainable employment opportunities. Support has been included for supporting infrastructure, active and public transport options as part of the Draft Plan. The County Development plan review is on the horizon and will address wider housing requirements for the County. The Committee requested that the presentation be circulated post meeting.

## Item 4: Update on Athlone Joint Urban Area Plan {#item-4}

Cathaldus Hartin provided an update on the joint urban area plan process with Roscommon County Council - one of the first of its type in Ireland. The Chairperson requested indicative timelines for when the draft plan would come forward to members. Cllr Farrell raised serious traffic concerns in Athlone and questioned whether provisions would be made for infrastructure such as a ring road. Cathaldus Hartin confirmed December 2025 for member consultation and advised that while no specific schemes are confirmed, the local transport plan will identify required infrastructure enhancements including roads, active travel, public transport and network improvements.

## Item 5: Draft Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure Strategy {#item-5}

David Connolly, Director of Low Carbon Mobility for City Consultancy, and Kieran Collins, Director at KPMG, provided a comprehensive presentation on the draft Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure Strategy. Clir Jones highlighted community group challenges with procurement and cited successful private installation in Kinnegad. Cllr Gaffney raised extensive questions about real-world charging point numbers, impact of new housing developments, private sector role, grid capacity concerns, and commercial viability. James Bourke emphasized the importance of correct placement near amenities and grid capacity as a fundamental prerequisite. Alison Hough questioned grid capacity solutions and concerns about private partnership reliance in rural areas. Cllr Farrell raised seasonal tourism impacts and heavy vehicle charging infrastructure. On the proposal of a Committee member, seconded by Cllr Farrell, it was agreed to bring the draft strategy to the full Council meeting.

## Item 6: Update on Biodiversity Action Plan {#item-6}

Christina Sweeney, Biodiversity Officer, provided an update on the draft Biodiversity Action Plan currently in process. Christina Sweeney confirmed that the Chief Executive report will include all consultation feedback, the PPN was engaged and will be again, and SEA screening was completed with the plan screened out. The plan contains 43 actions aligned with the National Biodiversity Action Plan.

## Item 7: Update on Progress with Irish Rail and NTA - Killucan Station {#item-7}

Clir Jones provided an update on the feasibility study for reopening Killucan Railway Station. A pre-feasibility study on public transport needs for Killucan, Kinnegad and Hill of Down was completed in July 2024. A meeting was held on 31 March 2025 with Irish Rail CEO, and the transport lobby group submitted their response on 4 April 2025. Irish Rail's response was received on 14 May 2025. The response cited multiple reasons against reopening Killucan railway station, despite the study showing 150 people per day would use the station and many existing stations having lower usage numbers. The response was considered inadequate on multiple levels. Strong demand evidence exists including high private bus usage through Kinnegad during peak times, with full buses operating efficiently, demonstrating strong demand from north county residents. On the proposal of Clir Jones, seconded by Cllr Gaffney, it was agreed to bring the Killucan railway issue to the full Council meeting for discussion. The Committee also requested circulation of the original request and responses to all members for review.

## Item 8: Aon Gnéo Eile {#item-8}

Alison Hough raised concerns about the rejection of her agenda item on Trees and Hedgerows Policy, which had been developed by the PPN over one year. She referenced SPC Terms of Reference LC.6 stating all members can table agenda items and expressed concern about the six-month delay this represented, The Chairperson acknowledged the need for clear guidelines on motion submissions and proposed a review of procedures with advance submission deadlines to balance comprehensive discussion with time management. Jean Ryan, Director of Service, clarified that trees and hedgerows were identified for the September SPC in the January work programme. The Environmental SPC is developing a tree management policy for Council lands, and September will include three strands: Council tree management strategy, landowner duties under Road Traffic Act, and the PPN Trees and Hedgerows policy proposal. The September agenda will also include dereliction and vacancy updates. Next Meeting Arrangements: On the proposal of a Committee member, seconded by Cllr Farrell, it was agreed to hold the September meeting at TUS Athlone new Science building on 19 September 2025 at 10:00 AM, subject to venue availability.
