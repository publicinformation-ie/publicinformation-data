## Item 3 — Update from Ms. Celina Barrett, A/Director of Services {#item-3}

Ms. Celina Barrett, A/Director of Services, Transport, Mobility and Open Spaces gave the members the following update: Capital Projects: Maynooth Eastern Ring Road (MERR): The tender for the main contract is closed. The documents are gone to the Senior Council before submitting to An Coimisiun Pleanala. It is expected that confirmation of funding will be received from the Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage under the Local Infrastructure, Housing Activation Fund (LIHAF) before the end of 2025. R448 National Cycle Network Pilot: The work on this pilot scheme is almost complete with some amendments received and agreed at public meetings held by Kildare County Council with residents. Speed Limit Reviews: Kildare County Council have commenced the public consultation for the “Proposed 30kph Speed Limit in Residential Housing Estates Taking in charge (Jake’s Law). When the consultation period closes, it will be presented to the full council for approval. The members of the committee were informed that Sinead Martin, Road Safety Officer has been appointed, and she will make a presentation to the members at the December meeting. Ms Celina Barrett informed the members that €11m was received for the restoration maintenance. (50km of roads were restored in 2025). The road dressing for 2025 is now completed with 86km improved to a value of €1.5m. Public Lighting The committee members were informed that Killaree Lighting Services (KLS) are the Maintenance Contractor. Ms Celina Barrett advised the members that repairs to lights can be logged on the Deadsure system on the Council website. She advised the members that 80% of the 28,000 public lights in County Kildare have been replaced with LED lights under the Public Lighting Energy Efficiency Project (PLEEP). Machinery Yard/Salting Barn: All vehicles used by Kildare County Council are managed by the Machinery Yard. 12 new electric vehicles have now been added to the Kildare County Council fleet. Ms Celina Barrett advised the committee members that the Winter Maintenance Plan 2025-26 is in place. The first storm of the season, “Amy” is reflecting an orange warning and briefings are being held with Met Eireann to monitor the situation. Local Improvement Scheme (LIS): This scheme for the upgrade of non public roads have 9 locations in 2025 nearing completion. Active Travel: Royal Canal Greenway: The official opening took place on 28 September 2025. The completion of this greenway scheme means users can now cycle over 120km from Leixlip, County Kildare to Cloondara, Co. Longford alongside the Royal Canal. The scheme was funded by National Transport Authority (NTA) under the Active Travel Investment grants. Bus Shelters The National Transport Authority (NTA) have agreed locations in the County for Bus Shelters. Consent under Section\_38 of the Road Traffic Act 1994 has been completed and it is expected that the NTA will arrange to supply bus shelters in Quarter 1 2026. M4 Bus Corridor This project is near completion but amendments in legislation to permit buses use the hard should is still outstanding. Park and Share The Kildare National Road Office (NRO) are working on the design for a Park and Share facilities on the on the M9 and N7. The next phase will be a public consultation for the two locations. The members raised the following questions: Cllr Peter Melrose requested clarification on when will the estates that are not in the charge of Kildare County Council receive Jake’s Law signage. He questioned the reason for no bus shelters in stock in Ireland. He requested clarification on the legislation for buses on the hard shoulders. Mr Eamonn Mahoney requested clarification if the M4 Bus Corridor was designed for emergency vehicles. Cllr Noel Connolly requested an update on the park and share at Osberstown. Cllr Kevin Duffy requested confirmation on staffing resources for all the schemes/projects. Ms Celina Barrett, A/Director of Services advised the committee that estates taken in charge of Kildare County Council is at 80%. Building Control are dealing with the remaining 20%. Legacy estates can be more a case-by-case situation to take them in charge and that there will be a provision in Budget 2026 to deal with the 20% housing estates not in the charge of Kildare County Council. In respect of bus shelters, she said that this was under the control of the National Transport Authority (NTA) In respect of legislation for hard shoulder, this is expected by the beginning of 2026., Transport Infrastructure Ireland (TII) have worked on a operational model when vehicles break down. It will be a managed system with controlled signals at specific times depending on the flow of traffic. She advised that buses will not have the use of M4 Bus Corridor on a permanent basis, it will be a managed system. Ms. Celina Barrett advised the committee that a park and share is proposed on the N7 near Goffs. In respect of staff resource, she informed the committee members that changes have taken place in qualifications for the recruitment of assistant engineers and executive engineers which will allow Level 7 graduates to apply for engineering positions.

## Item 5 —Briefing on Electric Vehicle (EV) Infrastructure Strategy for Counties {#item-5}

Kildare, Louth, Meath and Wicklow. The Director of Net Zero Transport (Systra), Mr. Peter Weldon thanked the Cathaoirleach for the warm welcome to the Transport, Mobility and Open Spaces SPC meeting. He gave a detailed background on the work that he has being carrying out on the Draft Regional 4 Local Neighbourhood and Destination Electric Vehicle (EV) Charging Strategy. Mr. Weldon advised that SYSTRA and KPMG were commissioned to develop an EV Charging Infrastructure Strategy and Implementation Plan (Region 4) for the Counties of Kildare, Louth, Meath and Wicklow. Mr. Peter Weldon gave the members a comprehensive presentation on the following: 1. Aims and objectives of EV charging strategy e Align with national and local plans. e Identify the benefits to users and the broader community but to name a few. 2. Development of EV charging strategy e KPMG undertook commercial analysis and grid capacity analysis. e Four-phase, twelve-task methodology to develop the EV Charging Strategy 3. Current EV charging in County Kildare e EV charging demand modelling undertaken to identify priority settlements for further delivery of public EV charging infrastructure. e The EV charging Strategy recommends a tranche-based approach to EV charging delivery. 4. Delivery of EV charging in County Kildare up to 2030 e A demand led rollout for neighbourhood charging is recommended in the immediate term. e Types of charging (Destination charging, Neighbourhood charging to Multi- user need charging. e Other areas of delivery mechanisms such as Hotel charging, EV charging in public sector car parks. 5. Grid capacity assessment for County Kildare e Address high demand areas first. e Greater public sector support required as less commercially viable. e Fill identified gaps in the network. 6. Commercial analysis for EV charging e Kildare is estimated cost of €6.8-€9.5m e Region 4 estimated total investment costs (€20-€26m) 7. Implementation plan for EV Charging Strategy e Various stages- National level support 7 Ongoing monitoring » Tourism charging » Hotel based charging » Neighbourhood charging ~ Destination charging 8. Recommendation for EV Charging Strategy e National-level recommendations e County level recommendations 9. Additional information - benefits and risks e Reduction in greenhouse gas emissions e Air quality emissions benefits. e Reduction in noise-related impacts of road traffic e Maximising of private sector participation He informed the committee members that the ‘EV Charging Infrastructure Strategy 2022-2025 is supported by two charging delivery plans: 1. National Road Network EV Charging Plan 2024-2030 2. Regional and Local EV Charging Network Plan 2025-2030. The Cathaoirleach, Cllr Duffy complimented Mr. Peter Weldon on his comprehensive presentation on the Electric Vehicle (EV) Infrastructure Strategy. The A/Director of Services, Celina Barrett assured the committee members that Kildare County Council has worked closely with Systra on this strategy to date. The members raised the following questions: 1. Cllr Noel Connolly requested clarification on the following issues » Networking with housing estates » Any engagement with EV owners » Planning processing » Lamppost for EV charging is it an option » Why is EV charging so expensive in County Kildare? » Any plans to have EV points at Bus Terminals and Taxi Ranks 2. Cllr Peter Melrose requested clarification on the following issues: \>» Rational on different charging adapters (CCS \& CHAdeMO) » Planning issues with the current EU regulations Mr Weldon responded as follows: 1. He advised that they are not recommending low voltage in residential developments as this would place pressure on the grid. 2. He advised that certain developments provide high voltage like filing station which have proven to have no effect on the environment. 3. The location of EV points on lampposts is a good option, but the wire regulation is being amended to make this option. 4. Bus electrification is taking place as the National Transport Authority (NTA) have plans to decarbonise their bus fleets. 5. He advised the committee members that the National Framework is due to published by the end of 2025. 6. He informed the members that EV charging at Taxi ranks is not option but close to the rank is an option. The Cathaoirleach, Mr Kevin Duffy and the members of the committee complimented Mr Peter Weldon on his comprehensive presentation. Mr Peter Weldon concluded his presentation and left the meeting.

## Item 6 —Briefing on the Outdoor Recreation Plan {#item-6}

Mr. Simon Wallace, A/Senior Parks Superintendent was welcomed to the meeting. He proceeded with a presentation on the “Outdoor Recreation Plan”. The committee members were given a background to the recreation plan with the following stages: 1. Development Plan Process: Tender for consultants was issued in November 2024 with S3 Solutions appointed in January 2025. 2. Consultation (at various stakeholders). 3. Consultation Phases » School focus groups — May 25025. » Kildare Sports Partnership Board- June 2025 » Stakeholders interviewed with National and Regional stakeholders to name but a few. This plan has been examined under the following: e Current context e Strategic context e Consultation findings The findings showed that walking was the most popular form of recreation. In light of the findings under the above headings the SWOT analysis was carried out using the following key themes: Strength, VWWeakness, Opportunities, Threats Mr. Simon Wallace concluded the presentation informing the committee members of the following steps that are required from the draft recreation Plan to reach a final strategy: A. Development of an action plan — 20 actions plus. B. Appropriate Assessment and Environmental Impact Assessment Screening C. Final Strategy- Approval from Transport, Mobility and Open Spaces Strategic Policy Committee (SPC) D. Proposal to circulate for approval to the full Council in (Quarter 1) 2026. The Cathaoirleach, Clir Duffy and the members of the committee complimented Mr Simon Wallace on his presentation. The members raised the following questions: 1. Cllr Duffy question if the 38 actions be investigated as advised by the Consultants and to carry out a full screening process. 2. Cllr Melrose requested clarification if activities like running tracks were examined for this strategy. 3. Cllr Connelly questioned how the issue of volunteering could be overcome. Mr Simon Wallace responded as follows: 1. The findings of the actions from the consultant’s findings were actions that were overlapping so considering this, 20 plus actions will be sufficient to examine. 2. He advised that the preparation of the strategy is rural walking based with less sport specific. 3. The issue of volunteering could be overcome with training from the Sports Partnership Leadership committee.
