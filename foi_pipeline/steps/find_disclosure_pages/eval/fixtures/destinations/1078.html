Information about the NSSO

The National Shared Services Office (NSSO) is an Irish shared services provider for Human Resources, payroll and pension administration and finance services for Government Departments and Public Service Bodies. Our organisation is comprised of Employee Services, Finance Services, Corporate Business Services and Enterprise IT.

The NSSO has over 900 Civil Service staff based in six offices across Ireland.

Shared services is one of the major strategies for the Civil Service and a significant transformational change that arose from Government policy to introduce shared services that has been implemented since 2013.

In January 2018, the NSSO was established as a statutory office under the provisions of the National Shared Services Office Act 2017 to combine and deliver efficiencies and value in HR, Pensions, Payroll, and Financial Management transactions for its client public service bodies. Links to the relevant legislation are listed below:

  • NSSO Act 2017
  • NSSO Act 2017 Revised

The NSSO’s governance arrangements differ from other State Bodies and reflect its specific needs while seeking to align with wider public sector policy and best practice guidelines.

For further information about the NSSO, our business plans & strategies visit:

https://www.nsso.gov.ie/en/corporate-information/who-we-are/

Services provided or to be provided to the public

The National Shared Services Office is currently organised into 4 Divisions. Each Division is led by an Assistant Secretary and is a distinct functional unit, however there is also a substantial degree of co-operation and interaction between them.

An outline of each Division’s functions and responsibilities can be found under Who We Are on the NSSO website and also on the whodoeswhat.gov.ie as follows:

  • 1. Corporate Services Division
  • 2. Employee Services Division
  • 3. Enterprise IT
  • 4. Financial Management Services Division

Corporate Services

Corporate Services is an internal support service managing resources for the achievement of the NSSO’s strategies, ensuring compliance with a number of governance obligations and improving corporate policies, procedures and outcomes. Corporate Services is responsible for co-ordinating the implementation of corporate governance and controls to process transactions which account for a significant share for the NSSO’s clients’ expenditure.

Further details on the NSSO’s Corporate Governance can be found on the following link: https://www.nsso.gov.ie/en/corporate-information/corporate-governance/

Employee Services

Employee Services provides Human Resources, pensions and payroll shared services to client government departments and Public Service Bodies (PSBs) and is supported by customer relations and support services. There are 44 client PSBs receiving HR services and 54 client PSBs for payroll services. These services apply the Government’s HR, pension and payroll policies and procedures on behalf of its clients and all key decision making remains with the client in each department or PSB.

Further information on the NSSO’s services can be found in the links below:

https://www.nsso.gov.ie/en/services/log-into-hr-self-service/

https://www.nsso.gov.ie/en/services/access-the-payroll-portal/

Enterprise ICT Division

The Enterprise Information and Communications Technology (ICT) Division has a responsibility for driving innovation to deliver new digital opportunities for the NSSO. The Enterprise ICT function leads the ICT Strategy for the organisation, managing third-party service providers, provides ICT operations and support services for the business functions of the NSSO. The Enterprise ICT Division also oversees and project manages ICT projects and is also responsible for the identification and management of ICT risk for the NSSO.

Financial Management Services Division

Finance Shared Services provides core financial processing and reporting on behalf of the NSSO’s client PSBs and provides a full customer and technical support service and a single financial management platform with integrated end-to-end processes.

This service is in the process of being delivered under the Financial Management Shared Services Programme across four waves.

Once fully operational, the central government finance system will support approximately 50 client PSBs, centralising all financial processing activity and delivering standardisation in financial reporting.

Decision-making process for major policy proposals

The NSSO Boards and Committees oversee key milestone decision-making and delivery against project objectives, in line with the approved business case and Government policies. This includes advice and oversight in relation to the project status, risks and issues, any changes to scope, timings, resourcing, and the business case.

Where appropriate, these Boards and Committees escalate matters or decisions to other relevant forums or persons, such as the CEO of the NSSO. This includes decisions that are not in line with the approved business case, budgets and/or Government policies, or where material, staffing or public interest concerns are at issue.

For further information visit:

Financial information

The NSSO’s Finance Unit is a core element of the NSSO’s assurance framework. The unit provides financial management and guidance to the NSSO, including preparation and negotiation for the budget for the Vote of funds, the approval and monitoring of payables, analysis and reporting of income and expenditure, and preparation of relevant statutory reporting and the appropriation accounts. The unit also provides the Minister, the CEO and the Management Board with relevant financial management information, and with briefing materials as needed for Public Accounts Committee and other Oireachtas committee hearings.

The latest financial accounts for the NSSO can be found on the Officer of the Comptroller and Auditor General’s website. For further information visit:

https://www.audit.gov.ie/en/find-report/publications/2025/vote-18-national-shared-services-office.pdf

Procurement

The NSSO Procurement Policy and related procedures are in place and communicated to staff in all business areas. Arrangements are designed to ensure compliance with EU and national guidelines, and with agreed NSSO budgets and business plans.

All employees are required to adhere to the guidance set out in the NSSO Procurement Policy. A nominated Procurement Officer oversees compliance, liaises with the Office of Government Procurement (OGP) in order to run fully compliant procurement competitions. The Procurement Officer also provides advice and support to the Contract Coordinator in each business area, who ensures that procurement for that area complies with agreed policy and procedures. A schedule of delegated authority limits is in place for the approval of spending, and prescribed procedures guide the process to be followed for each level and type of spending.

The OGP also supports the NSSO by keeping us updated on legal and policy obligations related to public procurement.
